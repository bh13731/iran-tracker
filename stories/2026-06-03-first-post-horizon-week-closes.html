What a Weekly Cycle Is — And Isn’t

The post-horizon regime has no published weekly clock. There is no scheduled rule reversal at T+168h, no architectural transition that fires on the seven-day mark, no methodology-page checkpoint that the regime is contractually obligated to publish. The May 28 reconvergence horizon dropped the comparator ladders, retired the rule-posterior-weights line, and collapsed the dual-rule rationale language to single-rule framing; from that point forward the only mechanical transitions on the calendar are Rule B-falsifying events (which require a separately-published methodology-page reversal) and the four pre-published measurement-quality landmarks on the forward calendar (ISW 50d on June 8, UAE 70d on June 18, IRGC Naval 60d on June 22, fire-silence Day 72 on June 22). The seven-day mark today is none of those things.

So why does the weekly cycle matter at all? Because the post-horizon regime is a calibrated publication: Rule B carries an implied per-day probability of a null calendar day (P(null) ~0.93), and the joint probability of any specific run length compounds against that calibration. The cliff-to-week-mark stretch is now fourteen days long; the post-horizon stretch is now seven days long. The first is a more aggressive calibration test than the second — 0.9314 ≈ 36% is at the margin of the modal evolution; 0.937 ≈ 60% sits squarely inside it. A regime that produces calibration tests is one whose published probability has a falsifiable handle. The week-mark is the natural calendar interval at which the calibration test compounds enough to be analytically informative without compounding so much that a single late-arriving event would re-invert the read.

The seven-day mark is also the first closed calendar interval the post-horizon regime has run end-to-end without operator discretion at any phase. The May 20 cliff fired on a published schedule. The May 21–24 natural-experiment window fired on a published schedule. The May 25 revision fired on a published schedule. The May 28 reconvergence horizon fired on a published schedule. From May 28 onward the only architectural transition available was the published forward calendar. The first weekly cycle under the simplified architecture closes today with the architecture having published through exactly as designed: no methodology updates, five measurement-quality landmarks crossed in sequence with the headline number rule-invariant across each, and the four-indicator watch list silent for seven consecutive days.

A Rule B per-day P(null) of ~0.93 implies that, conditional on Rule B being the correct generative model, seven consecutive null calendar days occur with frequency 60% over a random week and fourteen consecutive null calendar days occur with frequency 36% over a random fortnight. Both observed run lengths fit comfortably inside the modal evolution: the regime’s published probability is consistent with the observed signal stream, and the calibration is therefore not under acute revision pressure. Translated back into operational language: the post-horizon dashboard is doing what the methodology said it would do.

The week-mark also separates from the more interesting calibration question, which is the long-run rate. A 60% baseline at one week, a 36% baseline at two weeks, and a 13% baseline at three weeks. If the regime carries past June 10 (three weeks of post-horizon null calendar days) without a watch-list event, the calibration enters its first stretched-tail zone — observed signal would be less probable than a 1-in-8 modal evolution under Rule B, and the methodology would face increasing pressure to reconsider whether P(null) is genuinely 0.93 or whether the post-horizon regime has structurally lower event arrival rates than the cliff-era calibration assumed. That stretched-tail check is fourteen days away and is the regime’s first non-trivial calibration test.

For now, the first week sits inside the modal evolution. The methodology is operating as designed.

The Asymmetry Between Methodology-Rich and Methodology-Quiet Corridors

The May 20–31 corridor carried seven distinct calendar transitions: the cliff (May 20), the natural-experiment window (May 21–24), the revision (May 25), the reconvergence horizon (May 28), and three measurement-quality landmarks (May 29 UAE 50d, May 30 IRGC Naval lunar-month, May 31 fire-silence half-century). The methodology was structurally busy: every day on the calendar carried either an architectural-layer transition or a measurement-layer threshold that required publication discipline. The published dashboard updated multiple fields per day; the methodology page documented the procedures by which each transition would fire and what the post-transition state would look like; the rationale page shed roughly 40% of its line count without a single published number changing.

The May 28–Jun 3 corridor — the first full post-horizon week — carries the opposite signature. Five measurement-quality landmarks, no methodology-quality transitions, zero watch-list events. The methodology is structurally quiet: every day on the calendar produces a publication, but the publication does not move any architectural layer. The published dashboard updates only the date, the cumulative null-day counter, and the marginal arithmetic on the calibration tests; the methodology page documents the same procedures it documented the day before; the rationale page line count does not change.

The methodology’s modal-evolution claim — that the post-horizon regime carries extended quiet stretches between methodology-quality transitions, with measurement-quality landmarks as the only published architectural-layer events — requires both corridors to carry empirical weight. Methodology-rich corridors test that the regime fires its architectural transitions cleanly on schedule when calendar thresholds are reached; methodology-quiet corridors test that the regime publishes through unchanged when calendar thresholds are not reached. A regime that only handles methodology-rich corridors is a regime that requires operator discretion to fill the spaces between transitions, which is exactly the failure mode the post-horizon architecture was designed to prevent. The first full post-horizon week closes today with the methodology-quiet behaviour cleanly demonstrated.

The Five-Day Countdown to ISW 50d

The next methodology-side observable on the published forward calendar is the ISW/FDD/LWJ Apr 19 posture anchor crossing the 50-day stale mark at 00:00 UTC June 8 — T+120h from today. The ISW anchor is currently at 45 days stale; the UAE Apr 9 cumulative anchor is at 55 days stale (crossed its own 50-day mark on May 29, the first measurement-quality landmark of the post-horizon regime). The ISW 50d mark on June 8 will be the second of the two authoritative anchors to cross the threshold and the fourth measurement-quality landmark of the regime overall.

The ISW 50d mark is structurally distinct from the prior three measurement-quality landmarks because of what it does to the depletion-side anchor-refresh procedure. The methodology pre-committed to a two-tier handling rule: inside the 50-day freshness window an authoritative refresh produces an automatic anchor swap-in (the chart endpoint redraws to the new value, the cumulative counter updates, the methodology page documents the swap as routine); past the 50-day mark an authoritative refresh produces a methodology-page footnote-with-decomposition (the refresh must be decomposed into “genuinely new events,” “reclassification,” and “reporting-method correction”; chart redraw is conditional on the decomposition; the cumulative counter does not automatically swap). The Apr 9 UAE anchor crossed this boundary on May 29; the Apr 19 ISW anchor crosses it on June 8.

The week-mark today and the ISW 50d mark on June 8 are therefore structurally linked: the regime is publishing through the methodology-quiet corridor between two adjacent measurement-quality landmarks, and the next landmark flips the depletion-side anchor-refresh architecture itself.

The frozen depletion tracker has been silent across the first post-horizon week in the conventional sense (no missile or drone fire, no Iran-direct strikes, no Gulf casualties), but it has been analytically active in the way the dual-tracker architecture is designed to handle: the conditional-liveness reading on Tracker A has continued to update daily as a derivative of Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario) (locked at 23%); the reconstitution plateau day count has incremented from Day 35 (May 28) to Day 41 (June 3); the ISW anchor staleness has incremented from 39 days (May 28) to 45 days (June 3); the UAE anchor staleness has incremented from 49 days (May 28) to 55 days (June 3). The first weekly cycle therefore added six days of plateau Day count, six days of ISW staleness, six days of UAE staleness, and zero authoritative posture revisions to the depletion-side state.

This is the operational meaning of “frozen but not empty.” The depletion clock is paused (Iran has not fired since Apr 11); the depletion data is preserved exactly as of the Apr 19 ISW baseline (~200 launchers, ~70% of pre-war BMs retained, ~40% of attack drones); but the calendar continues to advance against the Apr 19 anchor whether or not new authoritative posture revisions arrive. The reconstitution plateau Day 40 round-number landmark on June 2 was the first calendar landmark to fire from this advance under the post-horizon regime; the ISW 50d mark on June 8 will be the second. The first weekly cycle has therefore been the structural setup for the June 8 landmark, even though the cycle itself published no methodology-quality transition.
