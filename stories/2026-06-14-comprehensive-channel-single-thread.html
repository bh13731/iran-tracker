The Whole Dial Hangs on a Single Thread: Anatomy of the 7% Comprehensive Channel

June 14, 2026 — Day 68 Analysis Cease-fire Scenario anatomy Single-channel dial Landmark-free null day
For five consecutive days the dashboard crossed a round-number landmark — a procedural flip on June 8, fire-silence Day 60 on June 10, IRGC Naval Day 50 on June 11, reconstitution plateau Day 50 on June 12, the surprise budget’s first sub-30% read on June 13. June 14 crosses nothing. No clock among the six the regime tracks — war, ceasefire, fire-silence, plateau, post-horizon, anchor-pair — lands on a round number today. Under the methodology’s separation rule that is not a hole in the record; it is the regime’s modal observable, the extended quiet stretch Rule B’s per-day P(null) ≈ 0.93 says is the most likely thing to publish. P(Hormuz reopen 30d) = ~6.3% (Rule B, single live ladder), unchanged for the twenty-sixth consecutive day. A day with no new threshold is the right moment to stop watching the clocks and look at the dial itself — because the single most important fact about it is not a landmark at all. It is an arithmetic identity: 6.3% = 7% × 90%, and nothing else.

The Dial Is One Multiplication

P(reopen) is the sum over four scenarios of P(scenario) × P(open | scenario). Write the sum out and almost all of it vanishes:

Ninety-three percent of the probability mass — the entire Stalemate scenario and the entire Rupture scenario — contributes zero to the dial, because in both of those worlds the strait stays closed. (A rupture restarts the war; it does not open Hormuz. That is why Tracker A goes live on a rupture while P(reopen) does not rise.) The headline is carried, in full, by a single 7%-weight scenario at 90% conditional probability. The dial is not a blend of four paths to reopening. It is one path, scaled by how likely that path is to actually clear the strait if it happens.

Why this is the load-bearing fact. When the partial-arrangement scenario was evicted at the May 20 calendar cliff, it did not just drop 15 pp of scenario weight into Stalemate — it removed the second positive-side path. Before May 20 the dial had two threads it could hang from (a partial traffic arrangement and a comprehensive deal). Since May 20 it has had one. Every property of the dial that follows — its stability under nulls, its insulation from rupture risk, its fragility to upside surprise — is downstream of the fact that the positive side collapsed to a single channel.

Why P(open | comprehensive) Is 90%, Not 100%

The one number doing all the work deserves scrutiny. Why isn’t a comprehensive peace deal worth a near-certain reopening? Because “a deal is signed” and “commercial traffic returns to ~pre-war normal within the window” are not the same event. The blockade has two locks, and a deal is the key to only the first.

So the 90% is itself an upside-leaning judgment: it assumes that a deal serious enough to be called comprehensive carries IRGC stand-down with it and that shippers and insurers re-enter the strait quickly. The 10% it withholds is the gap between a diplomatic event and a logistical one. This matters because it means even the dial’s single thread is not a clean “deal → open” switch — it is “deal → (very likely) open,” and the residual is real.

What Would Have to Fire in the Jun 14–Jul 14 Window

For the one channel to contribute its 6.3 pp as an actual reopening rather than a probability-weighted expectation, a comprehensive deal has to be signed inside the rolling 30-day window. Today that window is June 14 – July 14, and the starting conditions are bleak for it:

  • No active negotiating track. Islamabad Round 2 was postponed indefinitely on April 22; there is no announced Round 3 date, no named host, no confirmed back-channel. A comprehensive deal in 30 days would have to start, accelerate, and conclude from a cold start.
  • No positive watch-list signal in 18 days. The comprehensive-deal channel is the lone surviving positive indicator on the four-item watch list, and it has not so much as flickered across the entire post-horizon regime. The other three operative indicators are negative-side (a fresh seizure, a rupture, or — weakly positive — a commercial-transit cluster).
  • An asymmetric credibility bar. Negative triggers pass through unfiltered; a single fresh IRGC seizure re-elevates rupture risk immediately. A positive trigger has to clear a credibility test (a real signing, not a communiqué) before it moves the dial. The dial can fall faster than it can rise.

That asymmetry is the honest reason the Comprehensive scenario sits at 7% and not higher, and why — with the dial resting entirely on it — ~6.3% is a low, sticky number rather than a coiled spring. The structural read of a single-channel dial is this: it is fragile to upside surprise and robust to the daily nulls. Only one event class in the world can lift it, and that class is the hardest to manufacture on a 30-day clock from a standing start; meanwhile, the steady drumbeat of quiet days does almost nothing to it.

Which Is Exactly Why Today’s Null Barely Touches It

The landmark-free day connects straight back to yesterday’s calibration story. The eighteenth consecutive post-horizon null deepens the surprise budget to 0.9318 ≈ 27% — the ninth consecutive sub-50% read, and, because nothing else fired today, the single most isolated data point yet in the running test of whether the 0.93 per-day null rate was set too high. That test keeps pulling the same direction: the stalemate looks stickier than the May-28 model priced.

But trace where a “stickier stalemate” update actually lands on the table above. More weight to Stalemate (70%, contributes 0 pp) has to come out of the other scenarios — and the only one that feeds the dial is the 7% Comprehensive thread. So eighteen days of compounding nulls do exactly one thing to P(reopen): they thin the single thread by a sub-point fraction, well inside the published ±8 pp band. The dial is robust to nulls because it is a single channel and that channel is the only thing the nulls can touch — and they can barely touch it.

The clean statement. On a day with no landmark, the fact worth stating is the dial’s anatomy: since the May 20 eviction, P(reopen) = 7% × 90% = 6.3% and nothing else, with 93% of the scenario mass (Stalemate + Rupture) contributing zero. That single-channel structure makes the headline fragile to one thing only — a comprehensive deal signed inside the rolling window, from a standing start with no active track — and robust to everything else, including the eighteen-deep null streak that is quietly re-rating the stalemate as stickier than priced. The dial holds at ~6.3% for the twenty-sixth straight day because the one thread it hangs from was not pulled today, and on current signals is unlikely to be pulled inside the window.
