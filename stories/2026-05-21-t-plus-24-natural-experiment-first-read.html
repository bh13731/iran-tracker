Quantifying the T+24h Bayesian Update

Treat the three rules as competing hypotheses with roughly uniform pre-cliff priors (P(C) ≈ P(A) ≈ P(B) ≈ 1/3). Each rule has an implied per-day probability of producing the observed null result in T+24h:

RuleImplied daily event rateP(null day | rule)Posterior weight at T+24h
C (asymmetric, rupture-weighted)Elevated negative-side, ~25–30% / day~70–75%~28%
A (proportional, base-rate)Mixed, ~15–20% / day~80–85%~33%
B (all-into-stalemate)Suppressed, ~5–10% / day~92–95%~39%

The posterior weights are approximate but the directional update is clean: from uniform 33/33/33 at the cliff, T+24h shifts the distribution roughly to 28/33/39 in favor of Rule B. That is mild — the methodology does not revise its primary read on a single day’s evidence — but it is real. Three more null days in the window would compound the update to roughly 18/30/52 by May 24 (each null day multiplies posterior weight by the rule’s P(null | rule)). At that point the pre-committed primary read would be defensibly revised from Rule C to Rule B.

The asymmetric structure matters here. Rule C and Rule A are not symmetric in their failure modes: Rule C predicts more negative-side events than the base rate; Rule A predicts at the base rate; Rule B predicts fewer. An empty window is therefore Bayesian evidence against C, neutral-to-mild on A (depending on what counts as “base rate” in the post-cliff regime), and corroborating for B. The methodology can revise from C toward B on null evidence in a way it cannot revise from C toward A, because A’s prediction sits between C’s and B’s.

The IRGC Naval Channel Is Now the Whole Discriminator

Of the seven observable event classes in the read-grid, four involve the IRGC Naval channel directly (new seizure, naval-incident cluster, isolated incident, vessel release) and a fifth (rupture-leaning posture revision) is most plausibly precipitated by a fresh IRGC event. The remaining two channels — authoritative posture revision and commercial-transit cluster — are slower-moving and have produced no signal for 30+ days. For the next 72 hours of the natural-experiment window, the IRGC Naval channel is the single sub-indicator on which the rule disambiguation effectively rests.

The Naval channel’s 29-day silence is itself notable. The Apr 22 seizures opened a leverage instrument that depreciates with use; the 29-day non-deployment now exceeds the typical demand-articulation window of prior Iranian seizures by a wide margin. Three structurally different readings are consistent with the silence: (i) Iran is holding the vessels as a passive bargaining anchor against a future Round 3 that will not be announced inside the window, (ii) Iran has lost domestic clarity on what to demand and is paralyzed, or (iii) the silence reflects a deliberate Rule B strategic posture in which Iran prefers the stalemate equilibrium to either escalation or capitulation. Each reading produces a different prediction for the next 72 hours, but all three are more consistent with continued silence than with a fresh cluster.

The Rule-Invariant Headline and the Rule-Dependent Underneath

The dashboard reads ~6.3% today, the same as yesterday. The Hormuz tracker is rule-invariant on its primary headline within ~1.1 pp across A, B, and C. That is a deliberate property of the pre-publication discipline: by pre-committing the three rules’ ladders under timestamp, the methodology ensured the headline wouldn’t move with rule choice. The headline thus carries low information about which rule the world is consistent with.

The underlying P(rupture-scenario) is not rule-invariant. Today’s rule-weighted posterior puts it at roughly 25% (a weighted average of 28% × 0.28 + 27% × 0.33 + 23% × 0.39 ≈ 25.5%), down from yesterday’s 28% under Rule C-as-primary. That ~2.5 pp move is the load-bearing daily update beneath the unchanged dial. Tracker A’s conditional liveness — the probability the depletion math becomes operationally relevant again inside the rolling window — has therefore softened from ~28% to ~25%. Three more null days compound this toward ~23–24%; a fresh naval cluster reverses the entire update and pushes conditional liveness toward 30%+.

What the Window Has Not Foreclosed

The first 24 hours are mild evidence; they are not a decision. Three asymmetries keep the window meaningfully open through May 24:

  • Single events carry asymmetric weight. Within the remaining 72 hours, one fresh IRGC vessel seizure would corroborate Rule C with more posterior weight than the cumulative null evidence from three additional silent days. The Bayesian update is asymmetric because Rule C’s implied event rate is far above Rule B’s, so an event is a high-information signal while a non-event is a low-information signal.
  • Anchor revisions are due. The ISW Apr 19 posture anchor is now 32 days stale; the UAE Apr 9 cumulative is 42 days stale. Both are well past their typical refresh cadence. A neutral posture revision from either source inside the window would corroborate Rule A and force a different revision pathway than the Rule C→B drift the null-day evidence is producing.
  • The window is structurally short. Beyond ~May 28 the three rules’ marginal predictions reconverge as the watch-list base rates dominate. The window is designed to be narrow precisely because the rules are most distinguishable when the scenario stack is freshly re-anchored; the discrimination power decays quickly.

The Decision Rule for May 22–25

The methodology’s pre-committed decision rule is concrete. If by 00:00 UTC May 25 the natural-experiment window closes with zero IRGC Naval events, zero rupture-leaning posture revisions, and no fresh negative-side cluster, the methodology revises primary from Rule C to Rule B. The dashboard headline (~6.3%) does not move; the published rationale changes from “Rule C, smoothed asymmetric” to “Rule B, all-into-stalemate.” P(rupture-scenario) updates from 28% to 23%. Tracker A’s conditional liveness updates from ~28% to ~23%.

If a fresh IRGC seizure or naval-incident cluster fires in the next 72 hours, the methodology holds Rule C and updates the underlying rupture-scenario weight toward 30%+. If a neutral anchor revision arrives, the methodology revises toward Rule A. If direct Iran fire resumes, all three rules are superseded and Tracker A becomes operationally live.

The first 24 hours’ mild tilt toward Rule B is small but legible — the rule choice now matters less for what the dashboard says today (which is ~6.3% under any rule) and more for what the rationale page says by May 25.
