What Fired at 00:00 UTC

Two landmarks of distinct architectural classes fired on a single calendar transition. The first is a measurement-quality procedural-flip: the ISW Apr 19 posture anchor, frozen since Iran’s direct fire stopped on Apr 11, crossed exactly 50 days of staleness. Under the methodology’s pre-committed rule, the depletion-side anchor-refresh procedure transitioned in a single discrete step from automatic swap-in — a fresh ISW refresh redraws the depletion chart endpoint, updates the cumulative counter, and the methodology page documents the swap as routine — to methodology-page footnote-with-decomposition: the same refresh must now be decomposed into three components (a) genuinely new events since the anchor date, (b) reclassification of prior-anchor data, and (c) reporting-method correction, with chart-endpoint redraw conditional on (a) being non-zero and the cumulative counter no longer auto-updating.

The second landmark is a calibration-quality crossing: under Rule B’s implied per-day P(null) ~0.93, the joint probability of twelve consecutive post-horizon null days is 0.9312 ≈ 42% — the third consecutive day on which the observed all-null trajectory is less likely than not under the regime’s own calibration. The June 6 crossing (0.9310 ≈ 48%) was the regime’s first sub-50% calibration read and the structural opening of the asymmetric surprise budget; June 7 compounded that read to 45%; today compounds it to 42%. Each additional null day costs approximately three percentage points downward on the baseline — the compounding rate is invariant, but its operational meaning compounds with calendar exposure.

Why the co-location is the post-horizon regime’s sharpest dial-invariance test yet. The Depletion-Side Procedural Flip vs the Hormuz-Side One

The May 29 UAE crossing and the June 8 ISW crossing fire under the same methodology rule, but the post-flip procedure they produce is structurally distinct because the two anchors answer different questions. The UAE Apr 9 anchor is a cumulative tally: it asks “how many things have we counted across the war.” Its decomposition components are event-side — events missed in the prior tally, reclassified events, and reporting-rule corrections. The ISW Apr 19 anchor is a posture estimate: it asks “how many things does Iran have at this point in time.” Its decomposition components are methodology-side — events since the anchor date, underlying counting-rule revisions, and source-mix changes.
