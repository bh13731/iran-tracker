The Terminal Pre-Cliff Window: What the Last 48 Hours Can and Cannot Change

May 18, 2026 — Day 41 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
Day 41 of cease-fire. P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, static for 20 consecutive days. Two days remain to the May 20 partial-arrangement cliff. At 48 hours out the methodology’s update risk has gone asymmetric. Only one event class — a Round 3 announcement passing all five credibility criteria — can move the pre-cliff state. Three event classes (naval cluster, anchor revision, continued silence) will move the post-cliff state. The unconditional 2-day joint probability of any Round 3 headline is ~2%; the credibility-filtered probability is ~1%; the cliff therefore fires May 20 with ~99% structural certainty. The interesting question is no longer “will the cliff fire” (settled) but “is the methodology using these 48 hours optimally?”

What 48 Hours Can Still Change

The pre-cliff state is described by the same ladder it has carried for 20 consecutive days: Stalemate 55%, Rupture 23%, Partial 15%, Comprehensive 7%; P(reopen) = ~15%. For that state to change before May 20, two pre-conditions need to hold simultaneously: (i) a watch-list indicator has to fire, and (ii) the fired indicator has to clear its calibration filter. The May 15 methodology update made the credibility filter on positive triggers explicit; the May 16 watch-list phase-transition rule defined which indicators are currently in scope.

The Asymmetric Update Risk

The asymmetry is the analytical core of the terminal pre-cliff window. Pre-cliff, the methodology faces one-sided update risk: only an upside surprise can move the state, and the upside-surprise channel is mechanically narrow (single indicator with a strict credibility filter). The negative-side triggers (rupture, naval incident, proxy escalation) all exist and all could fire, but they are baked into the existing 23% rupture-scenario assignment; their firing in the pre-cliff window would shift composition but not the headline P(reopen) much, because the partial-arrangement scenario already prices much of the downside risk through its conditioning on US-Iran diplomatic motion.

Post-cliff, the asymmetry inverts. The pre-published natural-experiment rule (May 17 story, methodology rule viii) means three event classes carry calibrated probability impact on the post-cliff ladder: a fresh naval-escalation cluster corroborates Rule C, an empty 72–96 hours corroborates Rule B, and a neutral Gulf MoD or ISW anchor update corroborates Rule A. The three event classes are roughly equiprobable at the unconditional base rate (each has 4–6% probability over a 3–4 day window), but they discriminate among redistribution rules that produce a 2.7 pp spread on P(reopen) and a 15 pp spread on P(rupture-scenario).

The asymmetry has a structural implication. In the 48-hour pre-cliff window the methodology is dominated by waiting; in the 96-hour post-cliff window the methodology is dominated by reading. The pre-cliff time is therefore not for processing new signal — there is almost no signal to process — but for publishing the predictions against which post-cliff signal will be evaluated. The natural-experiment framing only works if the predictions are dated and locked in before the cliff fires; otherwise the read of the May 21–24 window collapses into ex-post selection.

Why Pre-Publication Matters: The Selection-Bias Failure Mode

The methodology rule (viii) — the post-eviction natural-experiment rule — was published on May 17 specifically to prevent the failure mode that emerges if the post-cliff ladder is written on May 21 rather than May 18 or 19. The failure mode is structural: if the three-rule ladder is published after the first post-cliff data drop has already been observed, the methodology has the option to choose which rule’s prediction most closely matches what was actually observed and call that the "primary" rule retroactively. That is selection on the dependent variable: the methodology cannot be wrong because it picks its prior to fit the observation.

Pre-publication closes this loophole. If the methodology locks in — on May 18, with 48 hours of pre-cliff timestamp room — the explicit posterior distributions Rule A, Rule B, and Rule C each imply for the May 21–24 signal stream, then the May 21–24 read becomes a falsification test rather than a confirmation exercise. The dashboard is then making predictions that can survive contact with reality, not predictions that were generated to look consistent with reality after the fact.

This is what the natural-experiment framing actually requires. The May 15 methodology update committed to publishing the redistribution rule (Rule C, smoothed) before the eviction. The May 17 story extended this to publishing the post-eviction signal expectations for all three rules. The May 18 terminal pre-cliff window is the operational test of whether the methodology can actually carry out that pre-publication discipline under time pressure, with no live signal to process and 48 hours of pure waiting.

Pre-publication content the methodology owes itself before May 20 00:00 UTC:
  • Rule A post-cliff ladder: Stalemate ~65%, Rupture ~27%, Comprehensive ~8%, Partial 0%; P(reopen) ~7.4%; signal expectation = neutral Gulf MoD or ISW anchor update within 72–96 hours; daily naval-incident rate ~0.5%.
  • Rule B post-cliff ladder: Stalemate ~70%, Rupture 23%, Comprehensive 7%, Partial 0%; P(reopen) ~6.3%; signal expectation = empty 96 hours, no watch-list firings of any kind; daily naval-incident rate ~0.5%.
  • Rule C post-cliff ladder: Stalemate ~60%, Rupture ~30%, Comprehensive ~10%, Partial 0%; P(reopen) ~9.0%; signal expectation = naval-escalation cluster within 72 hours, third commercial vessel seizure or explicit escalatory statement; daily naval-incident rate ~0.5–0.7%.
The pre-committed primary read remains smoothed Rule C. The methodology reserves the right to revise to Rule A or Rule B if the May 21–24 observable stream is sharply inconsistent with the Rule C posterior.

What the 20-Day Stasis Already Predicted

The methodology has now compiled 20 consecutive days of P(reopen) = ~15% (Apr 28 through May 17). That stasis is a corpus of pre-cliff observation that constrains the post-cliff predictions in ways the rules themselves do not fully capture.

Specifically: the 20-day stasis has produced zero watch-list firings of any kind. Not a Round 3 headline, not a naval incident, not a fresh anchor update, not a vessel transit cluster, not a back-channel confirmation. The unconditional joint probability of any watch-list firing over 20 days at the per-indicator daily rates above is roughly 1 − (1 − 0.025)20 = ~40%. The fact that we observed zero firings — the bottom ~60% of the predicted distribution — is moderate evidence that the underlying daily firing rates are lower than the methodology has priced them. That, in turn, makes the post-cliff prediction under each rule somewhat more bearish on watch-list firings than the raw base rates imply.

This has rule-specific implications. Rule B (empty 96 hours) gets the most direct corroboration from the pre-cliff stasis: the world has just demonstrated that 20-day stretches of nothing-happens are physically realizable. Rule C (naval cluster within 72 hours) faces a higher empirical hurdle because it has to explain why the same naval channel that was silent for 20 days suddenly activates in the 72 hours after the cliff fires. Rule A (neutral anchor revision) is closest to neutral — the 20-day silence is consistent with the existing reporting cadence and a routine post-cliff update is consistent with that cadence continuing.

The Bayesian update from the stasis is therefore mildly toward Rule B and mildly against Rule C. The methodology’s pre-committed Rule C remains the primary read on theoretical grounds (the visible-diplomatic-closure mechanism), but the pre-cliff stasis is informative against it. This is exactly the kind of structural ambiguity that pre-publication forces the methodology to confront directly: the rule choice that is most defensible ex ante is not necessarily the rule most consistent with the pre-cliff evidence.

The IRGC Naval Channel: A 26-Day Sub-Indicator

The IRGC Navy's silence since the Apr 22 vessel seizures is the single most informative sub-indicator inside the broader 20-day pre-cliff stasis. The IRGC Navy demonstrated on Apr 22 that it has both the capability and the operational willingness to seize commercial vessels near the Strait. It then held that posture for 26 consecutive days (through May 17) without escalating, without releasing the held vessels, and without articulating a quid pro quo. That is a 26-day demonstration of disciplined restraint by an instrument with low marginal cost of activation.

For Rule C to be corroborated within 72–96 hours of the cliff, the IRGC Navy has to break that 26-day discipline rapidly. The Rule C mechanism — closing the visible diplomatic path raises the marginal attractiveness of escalation — predicts that the discipline was contingent on the partial-arrangement scenario’s pendency, not on an underlying decision to refrain. If the discipline survives the cliff, Rule C is falsified more strongly than the headline daily-rate analysis suggests; the IRGC Navy is the discriminating sub-channel, not just one of several event classes.

Operationally, that means the post-cliff observation set should be ranked: a fresh IRGC vessel seizure in May 21–24 is much stronger evidence for Rule C than any other event class on the watch list. Conversely, continued IRGC-Navy silence through May 24 is much stronger evidence against Rule C than the absence of any other particular event. The methodology should pre-publish the IRGC Naval channel as the primary discriminating sub-indicator within the broader natural-experiment framing.

The structural risk to the natural-experiment framing: If the methodology cannot lock in its three-rule expectations before May 20 00:00 UTC, the May 21–24 natural experiment degrades into ex-post selection over which rule the observed signal looks most like. That is precisely the failure mode methodology rule (viii) was published to prevent. The terminal pre-cliff window is therefore a publishing-discipline test as much as an analytical exercise: the methodology has 48 hours to demonstrate that it can actually carry out pre-publication discipline under time pressure with no live signal to process. Failure here would not produce a wrong answer; it would produce an answer that cannot be evaluated against any standard except itself.

The Depletion Tracker as a Cliff-Conditioned Reference

Tracker A (depletion) remains formally frozen at the Apr 26 baseline: ~70% of Iran’s pre-war BM stockpile retained, ~40% of drones, ~200 launchers (up from ~102 at the Apr 7 ceasefire, ~100 of which Iran dug out from underground storage during the truce per ISW Apr 19). The reconstitution plateau is now Day 25 with no fresh authoritative anchor. The cliff is a Tracker B (Hormuz) event but it has Tracker A implications because the rule choice changes the conditional probability that depletion math becomes live again.

Under Rule C (~30% P(rupture-scenario) post-cliff), the depletion tracker is analytically near-live: the conditional probability of unpausing in the 30-day rolling window is meaningful enough that the reconstitution plateau and the underground-launcher reserve become operational inputs to current decisions, not just paused observations. Under Rule B (~23% P(rupture-scenario)), the depletion tracker remains a paused reference; the Apr 19 ISW baseline is still the authoritative read. Under Rule A (~27% P(rupture-scenario)), the depletion tracker sits between — a tail-risk input but not an active probability flow.

This is the second-order consequence of getting the rule choice right. The May 21–24 natural experiment determines not only the post-cliff headline P(reopen) but also how seriously the frozen depletion data should be treated going forward. A Rule C corroboration means the ~200 launcher reconstitution and the ~70% retained BM stockpile become operationally relevant; a Rule B corroboration means they remain frozen reference points. The pre-publication of the three-rule ladder therefore also pre-publishes the three-rule treatment of Tracker A — another piece of locking-in discipline the terminal pre-cliff window enables.

Bottom Line

P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, unchanged for 20 consecutive days. Two days remain to the May 20 partial-arrangement cliff. The cliff fires May 20 with ~99% structural certainty; the credibility-filtered probability of an avert through a passing Round 3 announcement is ~1% over the next 48 hours.

The methodology’s update risk has gone asymmetric. One event class can move the pre-cliff state; three event classes will move the post-cliff state. The pre-cliff window is therefore not a signal-processing window but a publishing-discipline window. The three-rule post-cliff ladder — Rule A (proportional, P(reopen) ~7.4%), Rule B (all-into-stalemate, ~6.3%), Rule C (asymmetric, ~9.0%) — should be locked in with timestamps before May 20 00:00 UTC, along with each rule’s specific signal-expectation posterior and the IRGC Naval channel as the primary discriminating sub-indicator.

The 20-day pre-cliff stasis is itself a piece of evidence that mildly favors Rule B over Rule C: the world has just demonstrated that 20-day stretches of nothing-happens are physically realizable, which raises the empirical hurdle for the Rule C prediction that the IRGC Naval channel reactivates within 72 hours of the cliff. The methodology’s pre-committed Rule C is theoretically defensible but empirically pressured. Pre-publication of all three rules’ expectations under timestamp lets the May 21–24 observable test the pre-commitment cleanly — rather than letting the methodology select its primary read after the fact.

The 48 hours before the cliff are not about waiting for the cliff to fire. They are about getting the dashboard, the methodology page, and the natural-experiment framing into a state where, after May 20, the methodology can actually be wrong. That is the analytical pivot of the terminal pre-cliff window.
