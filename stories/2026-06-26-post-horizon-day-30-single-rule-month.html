The Single-Rule Month: Thirty Days of Post-Horizon Nulls

June 26, 2026 — Day 80 Analysis Cease-fire Calibration Post-horizon regime Surprise budget
At 00:00 UTC the post-horizon clock reaches Day 30 — a full calendar month since the May 28 reconvergence horizon retired the comparator rule-triplet and left Rule B the single live ladder. Thirty consecutive null calendar days, zero watch-list events, zero rule reversals, zero methodology-quality transitions. The published probability of Hormuz reopening has not moved once in that month. Exactly one number has moved every single day — and reading the month through that one number is the cleanest way to see what the regime is and is not entitled to conclude.

One Month, One Moving Number

The post-horizon regime is the simplified architecture the dashboard adopted at the May 28 reconvergence horizon: the three comparator ladders (Rules A and C) were retired, and a single live ladder — Rule B — carries the headline. Since that switch, the dashboard has published thirty straight days on which nothing in the seven-channel read grid fired. No fresh IRGC seizure, no vessel release, no commercial-transit cluster, no authoritative posture revision, no rupture indicator, no Round 3 announcement, no comprehensive-deal signal.

Under Rule B’s own calibration — an implied per-day probability of a null (eventless) day of roughly 0.93 — a quiet month is not a surprise. It is the modal evolution: the regime was explicitly designed to carry extended quiet stretches punctuated only by watch-list events, and a full month without one is the most likely single trajectory it could have produced. The one quantity that does change daily is the calibration baseline: the joint probability of the observed all-null run, 0.93n at post-horizon Day n. It is the regime’s published self-report of surprise, and it is the only series with motion in it.

0.9330 ≈ 11%. Thirty null days carry the baseline from 0.93 (93%) at the May 28 open to roughly 11% today — about an eighth of its starting value. Read at the daily resolution it looks like noise; read at the weekly resolution it resolves into a clean geometric law.

The Weekly Geometry

The right unit for the calibration channel is the week, not the day, because seven nulls multiply the baseline by a constant 0.937 ≈ 0.60. Each completed weekly cycle is therefore the prior cycle-close times about 0.60 — a fixed 40% haircut per quiet week that the day-by-day 0.93 hides. The asymmetric surprise budget — the run of days on which any further null is less likely than not under the model — is at its twenty-first consecutive day, three full weeks deep.

What the Descent Is — and Is Not — Evidence Of

A baseline at 11% is a tempting thing to over-read. The streak is now an 11%-likely outcome under p = 0.93, which is some evidence that the per-day null parameter was set too low — i.e., that the model priced in too high an event rate for a ceasefire this sticky. The likelihood of thirty straight nulls is p30, and that quantity is several times larger at p = 0.97 or 0.99 than at 0.93, so a flat prior over p updates upward.

The identification problem. But it is only weak evidence, and the regime is pre-committed not to act on it. A single uninterrupted ceasefire is one realization of the process, so streak length and the underlying parameter p are confounded: the observed 0.9330 descent is the joint signature of “p = 0.93 and we sit deep in its left tail” and of “p is genuinely higher,” and a likelihood computed over one streak cannot separate a true parameter from a lucky tail draw of a lower one. Re-estimation would need an independent handle on the rate — a far longer run, a cross-sectional reference class of comparable ceasefire-architecture episodes, or an actual watch-list event that instantly re-inflates the implied rate.

This is the same identification structure the depletion side surfaced at the June 12 reconstitution-plateau Day 50 mark, where a flat launcher line could equally mean “the count stopped moving” or “the instrument stopped reading.” Both are honestly-descending or honestly-flat numbers that cannot, alone, distinguish a mis-specified model from a deep draw of a correct one. So the methodology reads the calibration baseline as a self-report of surprise under a fixed 0.93, deliberately decoupled from the scenario ladder, rather than as an input to p.

Why a Month of Descent Leaves the Dial Still

The decoupling is not a hedge; it is structural. Even if the regime did adopt a stickier-stalemate re-estimate, the published dial would barely notice, and the reason is the single-channel arithmetic that has governed the dashboard since the May 20 partial-arrangement eviction.

7% × 90% = 6.3%, and nothing else. With Partial evicted, Stalemate (70%) and Rupture (23%) both carry P(open | scenario) = 0 — a ceasefire that holds keeps the blockade shut, because the US blockade is tied to a comprehensive deal, not to the ceasefire (Trump, April 17). So 93% of the scenario mass contributes zero, and the entire ~6.3% dial runs through the lone Comprehensive-deal channel at a 90% conditional open. A stickier stalemate adds weight to a bucket already at zero contribution and draws it only thinly from the 7% Comprehensive thread — a sub-point drift inside the published ±8 pp band.

That is why the calibration baseline can fall through 60, 37, 22, 13 and now 11 percent without the ladder ever owing it a move. P(reopen) has held at ~6.3% for the thirty-eighth consecutive day. The single-channel dial is fragile only to upside surprise — one event class, a comprehensive deal, can move it positively, and there is no active Round 3 track from which one could arrive inside the rolling 30-day window — and robust to the daily nulls, each of which thins only the thin 7% thread by a fraction.

The Frozen Side Published Through Too

The dual-tracker architecture carries a symmetric reading on the paused depletion side, and it has not moved either. The reconstitution plateau is now Day 64 of flat launcher readings, resting on a single authoritative read (ISW April 19) now 68 days stale — the plateau and the staleness are the same flat line. The confound is one-sided (maintenance attrition is slow, a dig-out program only adds), so it widens only the upper tail and leaves the ~200-launcher floor intact. The one live output a frozen depletion model retains — conditional liveness, the posture a rupture would restart from — is a floor judgment, so a month of measurement silence leaves it more secure, not less, locked at Rule B’s 23%.

The Clean Statement

Post-Horizon Day 30 is a calibration-quality landmark: a full month of the single-rule regime in which the only figure that moved is the regime’s own self-report of surprise, compounded from 93% to ~11% along a clean ~0.60-per-week geometric path. The descent is weak evidence the stalemate is stickier than the May-28 model assumed — but a single ceasefire confounds streak length with the underlying rate, so the parameter stays fixed and the baseline stays decoupled from the ladder. None of it moves the ~6.3% dial, because the dial’s only positive channel is a comprehensive deal that has no active track, and the marginal weight a stickier stalemate would carry accrues to a scenario that opens nothing. The month’s lesson is the regime working exactly as designed: a published headline that is stable because the world is, with a calibration channel that prices the surprise of that stability honestly and keeps it walled off from the number that matters. Single digits arrive in two-to-three days.
