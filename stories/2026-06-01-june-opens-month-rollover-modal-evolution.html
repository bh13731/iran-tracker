The Two Corridors

The post-horizon regime’s claim about its own modal evolution is structurally compound. It says: under the post-eviction natural-experiment rule with the comparator triplet retired, the dashboard’s default state is to publish unchanged across long null stretches, with calibrated impacts firing only on watch-list events (fresh IRGC cluster, ceasefire rupture, commercial-transit cluster, comprehensive deal signal). This compound claim requires two distinct empirical demonstrations to carry weight: that the regime can publish through methodology-rich corridors without operator discretion, and that it can publish through methodology-quiet corridors with the same architectural stability.

The May 20–31 corridor is the first demonstration. Across twelve calendar days the dashboard architecture executed seven pre-committed transitions in sequence, ending in three consecutive measurement-quality landmarks compounded against unchanged headline numbers. By the end of the corridor the published P(reopen) had moved exactly once — the mechanical 8.7 pp drop on May 20 from the cliff’s scenario-eviction redistribution — and held the post-cliff value across the other eleven days. No subsequent transition (revision, horizon, three measurement-quality landmarks) moved the dial, the watch list, the ladder, or the rolling window’s width. The corridor demonstrated publication discipline under heavy methodology load: every pre-committed transition fired on schedule on a calendar trigger, and the rule-invariant headline carried through each one without operator discretion.

June 1 begins the second demonstration. The publication-month rollover is a calendar event without a methodology trigger; it carries no architectural transition; it does not even appear on the methodology-page’s scheduled-transitions calendar. The closest pre-existing concept on the methodology page is the rolling-window mechanic itself, which advances by one calendar day every midnight UTC anyway — June 1 is just the day on which that mechanical advance crosses a calendar-month boundary. The dashboard’s job today is to publish through that crossing with the same architectural stability it published through the methodology-rich corridor; the test is whether nothing changing is the regime’s default state, and whether the published numbers stay stable when no underlying signal is moving them.

The dashboard passes the June 1 test by carrying every value through unchanged: P(reopen) at ~6.3% for the thirteenth consecutive day; live ladder at 70/23/7/0; P(rupture-scenario) at Rule B’s 23%; Tracker A conditional liveness at the same 23%; four-indicator watch list unchanged; 30-day rolling window mechanically advanced by one day. The compound modal-evolution claim now has both endpoints — methodology-rich corridor and methodology-quiet day — and the regime is operating as designed across both.

That 42% baseline is the analytically load-bearing number for today’s read. It says: the twelve-day null stretch is approximately as likely under Rule B as a coin flip landing in its modal half. A reader looking at twelve days of unchanged headline numbers may form a prior that the tracker has stopped working, that the methodology is unresponsive to underlying conditions, or that some intervention is required to restart the dial. None of those readings survive contact with the per-day arithmetic. The corollary matters. If a future twelve-day null stretch occurs and is followed by a methodology revision or operator override that moves the headline, the operator action is informationally distinguishable from a signal-driven move. The regime’s published modal-evolution rate gives any future operator move a measurable distance from baseline: an operator-driven dial movement during a null stretch is a methodology failure, not a methodology output. The June 1 month rollover, by carrying through unchanged, defends the boundary between signal-driven and operator-driven dial movement at the publication-month transition where a less disciplined regime would have been most tempted to insert one.

Why the Rolling Window’s First Post-Cliff-Only Month Matters

The rolling 30-day window for Hormuz resolution advances by one day every midnight UTC. On May 20 the rolling window ran May 20–Jun 19; on May 21 it ran May 21–Jun 20; on May 30 it ran May 30–Jun 29; today it runs Jun 1–Jul 1. The mechanical advance is not the analytical pivot. What today carries that prior days did not is that every day in the rolling window is now post-cliff: the earliest day in today’s window is twelve days after the May 20 partial-arrangement eviction.

This matters for one specific scenario probability: the comprehensive-deal scenario, which carries the only non-zero P(open | scenario) on the single-rule ladder at 90%. The methodology has flagged the comprehensive-deal scenario’s 22-day central-case execution stack (announcement → negotiation track → signing → implementation start) since the May 14 pre-publication. A comprehensive deal that fires after the May 20 cliff and resolves Hormuz within the rolling window has to fit its 22-day stack into the window’s remaining days. On May 20 the rolling window had 30 days of forward exposure, all post-cliff; today June 1, the window still has 30 days of forward exposure, all post-cliff; the execution-window arithmetic has not changed. What has changed is that there is no longer a window-recent day on which the cliff is yet to fire — the rolling 30-day frame is now entirely a post-eviction frame.

The structural consequence is that the comprehensive-deal scenario’s 7% probability is now sitting against a backward-looking calendar with no pre-cliff history inside the rolling window for the first time. This does not move P(scenario) — the scenario probability is forward-looking and not affected by what fell out of the trailing edge. But it changes the comparative-information landscape: a reader looking at the dashboard today cannot find any day inside the rolling window during which the partial-arrangement scenario contributed to P(reopen). The cliff has now passed entirely into the rolling-window’s pre-history; June 1 is the first day on which the rolling window is structurally clean of the 15 pp scenario eviction that May 20 fired.

Why “Nothing Changing” Is the Output, Not the Absence of Output

The hardest part of publishing a low-event-rate stalemate tracker is the default-state communication problem. A tracker that publishes ~6.3% on day one, ~6.3% on day two, and ~6.3% on day three across an empty signal stream looks identical to a tracker that has stopped reading the signal stream at all. The publication discipline required to convey the difference — same number, different underlying reading — is the substantive cost of running a calibrated subjective Bayesian estimate during a stalemate.

The post-horizon regime addresses this problem by pre-committing to a single live ladder and a four-indicator watch list, then publishing the per-day P(null) implied by the watch-list base rates. The headline ~6.3% is rule-invariant under the published architecture; the compositional read on the 70% Stalemate scenario shifts daily under measurement-quality landmarks; the watch-list trigger probabilities update on each null day’s compound. The publication output on a null day is not just the unchanged headline — it is the unchanged headline plus the documented compositional update plus the rolling-window mechanical advance plus the calendar-landmark inventory plus the conditional-liveness reading on Tracker A. Five distinct pieces of information are published every day; only the first one (the headline) is rule-invariant by design.

On June 1 specifically: the headline carries through unchanged; the compositional read carries through unchanged (no underlying-indicator threshold today); the rolling window advances mechanically to Jun 1–Jul 1; the calendar-landmark inventory adds the new entry “first calendar-month rollover under the post-horizon regime”; the Tracker A conditional liveness holds at 23%. The publication output is not zero; it is the documented affirmation that the regime is operating as designed across the publication-month transition with no underlying signal moving against it. That affirmation is informationally distinct from silence, and the methodology has committed to publishing it on every null day rather than waiting for an event to publish.

June 1 carries through with no change to any frozen value.

The reconstitution plateau is now Day 39 and approaches the 40-day mark on June 2. This is the next calendar landmark on the depletion side of the dual tracker — not a methodology landmark, not a measurement-quality landmark, but a round-number recurrence on the most operationally load-bearing Tracker A indicator. The 40-day plateau on the reconstituted-launcher count carries a similar structural meaning to the fire-silence half-century mark on the Hormuz side: at 40 days of unchanged authoritative posture, the “tactical pause” reading on Iran’s launcher disposition becomes structurally weaker than the “stable equilibrium” reading. The Tracker A conditional liveness at Rule B’s 23% will hold across that mark; what shifts (analogous to the fire-silence half-century reading) is the compositional weight on the rupture scenario’s “Iran rebuilds and restarts on a calibrated timeline” component vs the “Iran maintains the reconstituted floor as posture and not as preparation” component.

The dual-tracker architecture keeps these two reads separable: the Hormuz side publishes through measurement-quality landmarks on a documented schedule; the depletion side carries the frozen state through the same landmarks with the conditional liveness explicitly tied to the Hormuz rule. June 1 is the first calendar day on which both sides are simultaneously publishing through a null transition with no underlying signal moving against either.
