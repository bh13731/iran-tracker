What a Plateau Landmark Means When Both Trackers Are Operating

The dual-tracker architecture is designed for ceasefires that look exactly like the one currently in operation. Tracker B (Hormuz) carries the live signal stream because the blockade is the active policy under which all observable Hormuz signals are generated; Tracker A (Depletion) carries the frozen state because Iran’s direct fire stopped on April 11 and the depletion clock is paused. But “frozen” on Tracker A is not the same as “empty.” The Tracker A snapshot still carries the ISW Apr 19 baseline (~70% of pre-war BMs retained, ~40% of attack drones, ~200 operational launchers); the conditional-liveness reading on Tracker A is still updated daily as a derivative of Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario) (locked at 23% under the post-horizon single-rule regime); and the calendar continues to advance against the Apr 19 anchor whether or not new authoritative posture revisions arrive.

Today is the first day on which the calendar advance produces a round-number landmark on Tracker A under the post-horizon regime. The May 20–31 corridor produced seven calendar transitions on the Hormuz side (cliff, natural-experiment window, revision, horizon, three measurement-quality landmarks), each tied to an underlying Hormuz indicator threshold. The depletion-side reconstitution plateau day count was incrementing through all of them — Day 27 on May 20 (cliff), Day 32 on May 25 (revision), Day 35 on May 28 (horizon), Day 38 on May 31 (fire-silence half-century), Day 39 on June 1 — but it had not yet crossed a round-number threshold under the post-horizon architecture. June 2 is when that landmark fires.

The 40-day mark is analytically load-bearing for a structurally specific reason: it is the first calendar window on the post-horizon Tracker A side that is long enough to distinguish between two interpretations of the same observation. A 10-day plateau is short enough that “Iran is still digging out underground storage at the post-Apr 19 pace” remains a coherent reading. A 20-day plateau is consistent with “Iran has paused dig-out to reorganize logistics.” A 30-day plateau is consistent with “Iran is testing what the ceasefire architecture will tolerate before resuming dig-out.” Past 40 days — a full calendar month of the post-Apr 19 reconstituted floor — the “tactical reconstitution pause” reading becomes structurally weaker than the “reconstituted equilibrium” reading: Iran has chosen to hold its launcher disposition at the ~200 mark rather than choosing to move through it.

Rule B’s 23% rupture scenario carries an internal composition that the published headline number does not separately publish. Under the “rebuilding for restart” component, the ~200-launcher floor is treated as an inflight value: Iran is paused at this count because the dig-out logistics or the political timing for a restart have not yet aligned, and a fresh authoritative revision moving the count upward (toward, say, ~250 or ~280) would be the leading indicator of a planned ceasefire rupture. Under the “reconstituted floor as posture” component, the ~200-launcher floor is treated as a terminal value under the current ceasefire architecture: Iran has rebuilt to the level it wants to hold for as long as the blockade-and-no-resumption equilibrium continues, and a fresh authoritative revision moving the count upward would be a meaningful escalation indicator (not just an in-progress data point).

The two components carry different implications for the rupture scenario’s probability mass even though they sum to the same 23%. Under the “rebuilding for restart” reading, the rupture-scenario probability is anchored by a forward calendar — restart fires when dig-out reaches a target, which makes the rupture trigger predictable conditional on observing the dig-out pace. Under the “reconstituted floor as posture” reading, the rupture-scenario probability is anchored by an external trigger — a US/Israeli action, a Hormuz incident, an Iranian domestic event — with the ~200-launcher floor as the starting condition rather than the lead indicator. Past Day 40 the implied prior weight on these two components shifts: every additional day at ~200 launchers makes the “in-progress dig-out” reading less coherent and the “steady-state posture” reading more coherent.

None of this moves the published number. The dual-tracker architecture is explicit about this: under the post-horizon single-rule regime the Tracker A conditional liveness is locked at Rule B’s 23%, and the only way to move it is a published methodology-page reversal. The plateau Day 40 landmark is the kind of compositional shift the methodology was designed to publish through unchanged: the headline carries through, the compositional read shifts, and the architecture documents the shift without requiring an operator dial movement.

The Symmetric Hormuz-Side Precedent (May 31)

The fire-silence half-century mark on May 31 was the first occasion on which a round-number calendar threshold landed on a Hormuz-side indicator under the post-horizon regime. The compositional shift it produced is the immediate analogue for today: at 50 days of fire-silence the Rule B 70% Stalemate scenario’s internal composition shifted from “extended tactical pause inside a war that may still resume” toward “Iran’s revealed strategic preference under the ceasefire architecture.” The headline P(reopen) did not move; the Stalemate scenario probability did not move; what moved was the internal weight on the two readings of why the Stalemate scenario sat at 70%.

Today’s plateau Day 40 mark fires the symmetric move on the depletion side: the Rule B 23% Rupture scenario’s internal composition shifts from “rebuilding for restart” toward “reconstituted floor as posture.” The headline P(reopen) does not move; the Rupture scenario probability does not move; what moves is the internal weight on the two readings of why the Rupture scenario sits at 23%. The dual-tracker architecture’s claim that measurement-quality landmarks publish through unchanged headline numbers while shifting compositional reads underneath is now demonstrated on both tracker sides through round-number calendar thresholds on the most operationally load-bearing indicator each tracker carries.

The symmetry matters. A tracker architecture that delivered compositional updates only on one side would be unbalanced — the Hormuz side would carry the analytical weight while the Depletion side functioned as a passive snapshot. The June 2 plateau mark demonstrates that the Tracker-A frozen state is itself an active reading: it generates calendar landmarks; it shifts compositional weights; it interacts with the Hormuz scenario ladder through the conditional-liveness reading. “Frozen” describes the depletion clock, not the tracker.

The Day 40 Threshold and the Two Anchor Stale Marks

The reconstitution plateau Day 40 mark interacts with the anchor-staleness calendar on the depletion side. The ISW Apr 19 posture anchor is the source of the ~200-launcher count; it is now 44 days stale (approaching the 50-day mark on June 8). The UAE Apr 9 cumulative anchor is 54 days stale. Both anchors sit simultaneously past the half-quarter freshness threshold for the fifth consecutive day. Under the published anchor-revision procedure, a fresh ISW or FDD/LWJ posture revision today would land as a methodology-page footnote rather than an automatic chart redraw, because the cumulative drift since Apr 19 now exceeds the freshness window during which an authoritative refresh produces a clean swap-in.

This is not arbitrary procedural overhead. The post-50-day footnote requirement exists because cumulative drift between authoritative sources accumulates non-linearly under ceasefires — the Apr 19 ISW figure was built against an active-fire signal stream and the Jun 2 read carries 44 days of ceasefire-period reporting drift on top of it. A fresh revision today would have to be decomposed into “genuinely new events” (post-Apr 19 dig-out, post-Apr 19 attrition), “reclassification” (Apr 19-era launchers re-counted on a different basis), and “reporting-method correction” (counting-methodology changes between Apr 19 and Jun 2). Inside the freshness window the decomposition can be handled in the chart redraw with a methodology-page note; past it, the decomposition itself becomes the published output.

The intersection of plateau Day 40 with anchor lag 44 days is therefore structurally informative. The plateau has held for 40 days across 44 days of post-anchor reporting; the 4-day gap (anchor lag minus plateau days) is the period inside the freshness window before the plateau formally began. From Day 1 (post-Apr 19) through Day 4 the ISW anchor was the dominant signal source. From Day 5 onward the Apr 19 figure has been the only authoritative anchor in operation and the plateau is the signal-substrate against which any future ISW refresh would be evaluated.

What a Fresh Authoritative Posture Revision Would Now Cost

The procedural cost of a hypothetical fresh ISW or FDD/LWJ posture revision today is the test of the post-horizon Tracker-A architecture. The methodology pre-committed to handling such a revision under two distinct procedures: inside the 50-day freshness window the revision is an automatic anchor swap-in (chart endpoint redraws to the new value; cumulative counter updates; methodology-page documents the swap as routine); past the 50-day mark the revision becomes a methodology-page footnote (the chart redraw is conditional on the decomposition; the cumulative counter does not automatically swap; the methodology page documents which components of the cumulative diff the methodology attributes to which source).

Today the ISW anchor is at 44 days stale, six days short of the 50-day footnote threshold on June 8. A revision arriving today (highly improbable; no ISW special report on Iranian posture since Apr 19) would still qualify for automatic swap-in. A revision arriving on or after June 8 would be the first refresh to require the footnote-with-decomposition procedure. The plateau Day 40 mark today is therefore analytically distinct from the ISW 50-day mark on June 8: the plateau mark is about what the held value means; the 50-day mark is about what would happen if the value were revised.

The two landmarks together produce the structural reading the dual-tracker architecture was built for: the plateau Day 40 reading is held with progressively less anchor freshness. By June 8 the plateau will be at Day 46 against an anchor at the 50-day footnote threshold. By June 18 (if no revision arrives) the plateau will be at Day 56 against an anchor at 60 days stale. Each day on the post-anchor-freshness side adds compositional weight on the “reconstituted equilibrium” reading at the cost of progressively-decaying confidence in the ~200 floor itself.
