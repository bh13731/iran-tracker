What the Window Actually Produced

Through 23:00 UTC May 24 (publication), the four-day window has logged zero events across every channel the May 19 read-grid enumerated:

  • IRGC Naval (seizures, releases, incidents): Day 32 of silence since the Apr 22 vessel seizures. Two ships still held; no resolution; no new seizure cluster; no commercial-transit interference incident.
  • Direct Iran fire (BMs, drones, CMs): Day 43 of silence since the Apr 11 pipeline salvos. This crosses 100% of the war’s 43-day pre-silence direct-fire interval today; tomorrow opens the period where fire-silence is for the first time longer than the active-fire phase that preceded it.
  • Authoritative posture revision (ISW / FDD / LWJ / Gulf MoD): Anchor lag 35 days since ISW/FDD/LWJ Apr 19; 45 days since UAE Apr 9 cumulative. No new tier-1 anchor in either direction across the window.
  • Commercial transit cluster: No multi-vessel reopening signal reported; BBC reporting of “few ships moving” through the Strait remains the operative reference.
  • Comprehensive deal signal: No Round 3 announcement, no leaked term sheet, no track-2 communique.
  • Ceasefire rupture indicator: No Iranian official walk-back, no US escalation order, no carrier movement beyond the existing three-CSG posture.
  • Proxy escalation cluster: No further Iraq-launched drones into Kuwait since the Apr 24 incident; no Hezbollah, Houthi, or Iraqi militia activity above the noise floor.

Seven channels × four calendar days = 28 channel-days of observation, all null. Under the read-grid the joint probability of this outcome under Rule C is ~24% (0.704); under Rule A ~48% (0.834); under Rule B ~75% (0.934). The likelihood ratios that produced the posterior shift are direct: Rule B’s implied four-day signature is ~3× as likely as Rule C’s under the empirical observation. The posterior reflects exactly that arithmetic.

What the Four-Day Window Has Demonstrated About the Methodology

The four-day window was framed under timestamp as a natural experiment. With the window now ~95% closed and the trajectory toward the predicted Rule B signature locked in, today is the analytical pivot at which the experiment’s outcomes for the methodology — rather than for any single forecast — can be read.

What the methodology got right. First, the May 19 baseline lock-in story pre-published the three-rule ladder, the per-day P(null | rule) read-grid, and the May 25 revision threshold — before any of the post-cliff data was observable. Every value used to compute today’s posterior is dated to a moment when the rule the evidence would support was not visible. Second, the May 17 first-post-cliff-observable story called the natural-experiment design correctly: the rules were identifiably different over the immediate post-cliff window, and the window was front-loaded as predicted, with successive observations buying less posterior movement. Third, the May 14 redistribution story’s observation that the rules’ P(reopen) band would be narrow (6.3–7.4%) but the P(rupture-scenario) band would be wide (23–28% in the realized window, vs the pre-cliff 23–38% theoretical) was load-bearing: the dial was always going to read approximately the same value; the rationale-page tail-risk read was where the rule choice would actually move things.

What the methodology got wrong, or hedged unnecessarily. One clean miss and one defensible hedge. The clean miss: Rule C was published as the primary read on the grounds that the May 14 redistribution story’s asymmetric-rupture-weighting argument was the most theoretically compelling redistribution; the four-day window’s empirical stream produced no support for that read. The defensible hedge: by holding Rule C primary through the full window rather than revising earlier, the methodology preserved the option value of a fresh IRGC event that would have inverted the posterior — an event that did not fire but that retained roughly 3–15% integrated probability across the window. The hedge cost was that the dashboard’s rationale-page tail-risk read was overstated by ~5 pp for four days; the hedge benefit was that the methodology was not exposed to the failure mode where it chases evidence and revises on the most empirically convenient date in hindsight.

What stays unsettled. The window has discriminated between Rule B and Rule C with high confidence (~3× likelihood ratio over four days), but it has not meaningfully discriminated Rule A from either. Rule A’s posterior has barely moved across the window because its per-day P(null) sits close to the geometric mean of the other two rules. The methodology revision at midnight will name Rule B as the new primary read; the next-most-likely revision target (Rule A) sits at ~32% posterior weight, well below Rule B’s ~52% but well above Rule C’s ~16%. The implied operational question for the next 7–14 days is whether a neutral anchor revision (ISW or Gulf MoD updating their cumulative figures or posture) clears the threshold to revisit Rule A, or whether the watch-list base rates reassert and the post-revision read stabilizes on Rule B.

The full chart series, the snapshot table, the days-to-zero projection, and the launcher-history visualization all remain on the dashboard at their last live state precisely because the methodology cannot rule out the ~23% Rule B rupture scenario, and a step move from 28% to 23% does not change the contingency architecture.
