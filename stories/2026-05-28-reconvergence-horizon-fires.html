Nothing in the published numbers. The mid-May 19 triplet read had Rules A/B/C at headline ~7.4% / ~6.3% / ~6.3% — a 1.1 pp spread that the methodology judged below the dashboard's smallest meaningful published increment. The horizon therefore could not move the headline regardless of which rule was selected as the surviving primary.

The Three Categories of Retired Content

The horizon retires content in three distinct categories. Each has a different reason for retirement and a different operational implication.

(i) The comparator scenario ladders. Rules A (proportional reweighting, 65/27/8/0 → 7.4%) and C (smoothed asymmetric, 65/28/7/0 → 6.3%) are removed from the rationale page. They served their published purpose: producing identifiably different posteriors over the May 21–24 natural-experiment window, allowing the methodology revision on May 25 to be mechanically driven by the observed signal rather than by operator interpretation. By the May 27 final triplet read, the rule-posterior weights were ~9/30/61 — Rule C’s remaining hypothesis mass had become a stranded asset, with its falsifying-event calendar exposure roughly 87% consumed. Carrying the comparator ladders past the horizon would have published numbers with no remaining informational role.

(ii) The rule-posterior-weights line. The single dashboard line that summarized the Bayesian update against the May 19 read-grid (~9/30/61 today) is retired. This is a stricter retirement than the comparator ladders: the comparator ladders documented the alternative scenario architectures, while the posterior-weights line documented the live arbitration between them. Without alternative scenarios on the page, there is no arbitration to publish. The posterior-weights line had no independent existence outside the triplet display.

(iii) The dual-rule rationale language. Watch-list indicator descriptions that carried both Rule B and Rule C framings — most prominently the IRGC Naval channel’s “corroboration under Rule C / falsification under Rule B” tag, introduced at the May 25 revision — are simplified to the Rule B framing only. This is a cosmetic retirement on the surface, but it has a substantive consequence (covered in the next section): the procedure for responding to a Rule B-falsifying event changes when the alternative rule is no longer on the page.

The Five Categories of Retained Content

The methodology was explicit at the May 17 publication of the post-eviction natural-experiment rule about what carried through the horizon and what did not. Five categories survive:

  • The four-scenario architecture. Stalemate / Rupture / Comprehensive / Partial. The partial scenario remains evicted (P(scenario) = 0%, P(open | scenario) = N/A); the four-scenario shape is the published primitive across pre-cliff, post-cliff, post-revision, and post-horizon dashboards. The scenario set is older than the rule-triplet structure and outlives it.
  • The four-indicator watch list. All four operative indicators (IRGC seizure, ceasefire rupture, commercial transit cluster, comprehensive deal signal) carry over with their calibrated probability impacts unchanged. The indicator framing simplifies; the indicator set and impact values do not. The May 20 watch-list phase transition (eviction of Round 3, mediation, partial-support, and back-channel indicators) was a substantive retirement; the May 28 simplification is a presentation change.
  • The 30-day rolling window. Advances daily as before. Today it shifts from May 27–Jun 26 to May 28–Jun 27, the same calendar-mechanical advance as every other publication day.
  • The Tracker A conditional-liveness reading. Locked at Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario) of 23% since the May 25 revision; unchanged at the horizon. The footnote on the depletion-tracker frozen banner simplifies from rule-triplet weighting to a Rule B-only reference, but the published 23% value does not change.
  • The full source-file documentation. source/hormuz-state.md retains the complete rule-triplet history, including the final pre-horizon weights (B ~61% / A ~30% / C ~9%), the comparator scenario ladders, the seven-channel read-grid, and the full natural-experiment timeline. The retirement is from the live dashboard only; the historical record is preserved.

The Reversal Procedure Changes

The substantive cost of the comparator drop is in the procedure for responding to a Rule B-falsifying event. During the triplet regime (May 25–27), a falsifying event — canonically a fresh IRGC seizure cluster — would produce an automatic in-rule posterior update: the posterior weights would shift back toward Rule C, the primary read might or might not flip depending on the magnitude of the update against the May 19 read-grid, and the dashboard rationale page would document the update on the existing rule-posterior-weights line. The whole protocol was visible on the dashboard and self-executing.

Under the post-horizon single-rule regime, the same falsifying event requires a heavier operator response. The dashboard does not publish a Rule C posterior to shift to; the rule-posterior-weights line is retired; the alternative scenario architecture is on the source-file side rather than the dashboard. A Rule B-falsifying event therefore requires a separately-published methodology-page reversal re-introducing Rule C as primary at the post-event posterior weights (or, in extremis, a new rule construction). The reversal is a discrete operator-published event, not an automatic Bayesian update against a standing read-grid.

This is the calibrated cost the methodology pre-committed at the May 17 publication: a simpler dashboard, at the price of a heavier reversal procedure. The trade-off pencils because the calendar exposure for a Rule C-corroborating event has already been roughly 87% consumed by May 27, and the marginal informational value of carrying the triplet structure past the horizon was below the threshold at which the dashboard signal-to-noise gain pays for the structural complexity. The methodology accepts the heavier reversal procedure as the price of dashboard simplification.

The Tracker A Boundary, Day 4

Today is the fourth full calendar day on which Iran’s fire-silence interval is longer than the active-fire phase that preceded it (Day 47 silence vs Day 43 active fire = 109% of pre-silence duration). The interval has now compounded to roughly 9% above the boundary — small in absolute terms, but the cumulative trajectory remains unambiguous: the silence is no longer interpretable as a tactical pause, and the longer it extends past the boundary the more it reads as Iran’s revealed strategic commitment under the ceasefire architecture.

The frozen tracker’s depletion math does not move at the horizon. Iran still retains ~70% of the pre-war BM stockpile and ~40% of the pre-war drone stockpile per the Apr 19 NYT/US officials read; the effective launcher count holds at ~200 (reconstitution plateau Day 35) per the Apr 19 ISW baseline; the days-to-functional-zero figure sits at ~100 from any future resumption. The chart and snapshot table remain on the dashboard at their last live state precisely because the methodology cannot rule out the ~23% Rule B rupture scenario inside the rolling window.

What changes at the horizon for Tracker A is purely the footnote on its conditional-liveness reading. The pre-horizon footnote referenced the rule-triplet weighting (e.g., “Tracker A conditional liveness at Rule B’s 23%, weighted posterior across A/B/C”). The post-horizon footnote simplifies to a Rule B-only reading (“Tracker A conditional liveness at Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario) = 23%”). The 23% figure does not change. The chart visualization does not change. The depletion-tracker frozen banner’s reference to the rule-triplet weights becomes a Rule B-only reference. Nothing operational moves.

What the Post-Horizon Steady State Looks Like

Three structural properties define the post-horizon dashboard equilibrium:

  1. Single-rule architecture. All dashboard reads are Rule B reads. Any subsequent published structural transition requires either a separately-published rule reversal triggered by a Rule B-falsifying event, or a fresh cliff event triggered by a calendar-threshold rule not yet on the methodology page. Neither is currently scheduled. The dashboard has shed the triplet’s self-arbitration machinery and now publishes one rule, audited against the source-file historical record rather than against a parallel live alternative.
  2. Static base-rate reads. Under Rule B’s implied per-day P(null) of ~0.93, the base-rate expectation is that the dashboard reads ~6.3% / 70/23/7/0 / 23% Tracker A conditional liveness for an extended period punctuated only by watch-list events. A two-week stretch without any watch-list event has joint probability of roughly ~38% under Rule B; the modal post-horizon evolution is a long quiet stretch. The dashboard now has fewer structural transitions on its calendar than at any point since the May 20 cliff was scheduled.
  3. Asymmetric event response. The four-indicator watch list is ~75% negative-side by trigger weight (three negative triggers, one positive). The post-horizon dashboard responds proportionally faster to negative events than to positive events — the calibrated consequence of the May 20 watch-list phase transition, preserved unchanged through the horizon. The asymmetric response was a substantive change at the May 20 cliff; the May 28 horizon does not change it.

The next non-base-rate event on the dashboard’s calendar is the Apr 9 UAE anchor crossing its 50-day stale mark tomorrow (May 29). That is a measurement-quality landmark, not a methodology landmark — it does not trigger a rule update or a structural transition. It is, however, the first dashboard observable on the calendar after the horizon, and an authoritative UAE cumulative revision would land against a 50-day reporting gap. The Apr 19 ISW anchor crosses 40 days tomorrow as well; the same quality flag applies. After those two crossings the published dashboard is in steady state until either a watch-list indicator fires or a new methodology threshold is published.
