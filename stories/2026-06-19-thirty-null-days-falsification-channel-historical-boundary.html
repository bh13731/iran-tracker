Two Counters, Off by One — and the One-Day Gap Is the Point

The dashboard carries two clocks that look like they should agree and don't. The dial has been unchanged for thirty-one days; the null-since-cliff counter reaches thirty today. The gap is not a rounding error — it is exactly the cliff day itself. On May 20 the partial-arrangement scenario evicted on schedule and the headline moved, dropping from ~15% to ~6.3% in a single mechanical step. That was the last day P(reopen) actually changed. The “unchanged” clock counts from that move (it has held at 6.3% ever since, including the day it arrived there); the “null since cliff” clock counts only the genuinely eventless days that followed the cliff.

So today's milestone is the cleaner of the two. “Unchanged for a month” is a statement about a number on a screen. “Thirty null days since the cliff” is a statement about the world: thirty consecutive calendar days on which not one of the seven enumerated read-grid channels produced an event. That is the figure worth interrogating — because a null is only as informative as the channel that stayed silent.

The seven channels are not equal. Under Rule B (all-into-stalemate, the single live ladder since the May 28 reconvergence horizon), three of the seven channels are negative-side triggers, three are retired or positive-only, and exactly one is the primary falsification indicator — the single observable that, if it fired, would break the all-into-stalemate read and force a separately-published rule reversal. That channel is the IRGC naval seizure clock. Its silence is doing more work than the other six combined.

Why the Naval Channel Is the Load-Bearing Null

When the partial-arrangement scenario was evicted on May 20 and the comparator rules were retired on May 28, the watch list collapsed to four operative indicators — and the dial's composition simplified to a stark form. The entire ~6.3% headline is now 7% × 90%: the lone Comprehensive-deal channel at a 90% conditional open, times its 7% scenario weight. Stalemate (70%) and Rupture (23%) both carry P(open)=0, so 93% of the scenario mass contributes nothing to the dial.

In that structure the IRGC naval channel is special. It does not feed the dial directly — it contributes 0 pp like the rest of the rupture-side mass. What it governs is the 23% rupture weight itself. A fresh seizure cluster would re-elevate the probability that the ceasefire ruptures, which under Rule B is the event that flips Tracker A live and falsifies the all-into-stalemate read. So the naval channel is the methodology's tripwire: not a contributor to the positive-side dial, but the indicator whose silence certifies that the 70/23 split is holding and whose noise would tear it open.

That is why a month of naval silence is not interchangeable with a month of, say, no-Round-3-announcement (a retired channel) or no-commercial-transit-cluster (a thin positive trigger). Thirty null days on the falsification channel is the strongest evidence the methodology can produce that the rupture scenario is not being actively pressured upward — the single most reassuring null on the board for anyone reading the stalemate as durable.

The Historical Boundary, 48 Hours Out

The Apr 22 seizure — two commercial vessels taken near the Strait — is now Day 58 of silence: 58 days with the ships held, no resolution, no release, no fresh seizure, no published quid pro quo. At ~8.3 weeks the episode has already outlasted the resolution windows of the two most recent comparable Gulf seizures. In 48 hours, at Day 60 on June 21, it crosses into a band that has historically meant something specific.

The base-rate logic is what makes the threshold informative. Early in a seizure's life, two priors compete: the seizure could be the opening salvo of an escalation pattern (more seizures, rising naval pressure, a slide toward rupture), or a single-event leverage trap (one grab, held indefinitely as a bargaining chip, no follow-on). The two priors are observationally similar in the first few weeks. They diverge as the hold lengthens with no second seizure: an escalation pattern produces more events; a leverage trap produces exactly this — one event, frozen. Past the MSC Aries/Niovi windows, and now entering the Stena Impero band, the weight of evidence has shifted decisively toward the leverage-trap reading. Sixty days of single-event silence is what a leverage trap looks like, and increasingly not what an escalation opener looks like.

Silent in both directions. The subtle point is that the Apr 22 seizure has produced no event of either sign. No fresh seizure (which would push toward rupture, falsifying Rule B). And no release (which would be a de-escalatory gesture, weak evidence toward a deal). The ships are held but undeployed — leverage Iran has acquired and is neither escalating nor cashing in. A frozen chip is exactly the signature of the 70% Stalemate scenario: capacity to disrupt, retained; willingness to either escalate or trade it away, absent.

What It Does — and Doesn't — Move

None of this moves the headline. The naval channel contributes 0 pp directly, so the dial stays at 7% × 90% = 6.3%, unchanged for the thirty-first consecutive day. The Day 60 crossing on June 21 will not move it either; only a comprehensive-deal signal can lift the dial, and only a fresh seizure (or an outright rupture) can drop it via the rupture path.

What a calendar month of naval silence moves is the compositional read beneath the split. It is the cleanest evidence the falsification channel can produce that Rule B is holding — that the 70% Stalemate weight is earned and the 23% rupture weight is not under upward pressure. And it has a mirror on the frozen tracker: the naval channel governs how likely a rupture is (the 23% weight); the depletion side's ~200-launcher floor (plateau Day 57, ISW anchor 61 days stale but floor-robust) governs what posture a rupture would restart from. Two halves of one conditional-liveness coin, one on each tracker, neither touching the dial.

The Clean Statement

Thirty null days since the cliff is not thirty interchangeable nothings. The seven read-grid channels carry unequal weight, and the load-bearing one — the IRGC naval falsification clock — has now stayed silent through every one of those thirty days and is two days from crossing into the Stena Impero ~10-week band, the duration at which a held-but-undeployed seizure stops reading as an escalation opener and starts reading as a single-event leverage trap.
