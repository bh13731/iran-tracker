The Calendar Erosion: When Does Absence of Signal Become a Signal?

May 9, 2026 — Day 32 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
Day 32. P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, static for 11 consecutive days (Apr 28–May 9). The tracker’s methodology holds probability fixed absent an explicit new signal — absence of news is treated as weak evidence of stalemate, not as a trigger for revision. But calendar compression is a qualitatively different kind of silence: not just “nothing happened,” but “the window for something to happen is getting shorter.” A structural audit of how much execution time the partial arrangement scenario has left, where the feasibility threshold is, and why May 20 is the date that turns calendar erosion from a passive background force into an analytically load-bearing signal.

The Methodology’s No-Decay Rule

The Hormuz tracker uses a subjective Bayesian approach with a specific update rule: probabilities change when new signals arrive. Absence of news is treated as weak evidence of continued stalemate — it provides a small nudge toward the 55% stalemate scenario, but not enough to move the point estimate within the tracker’s precision. The headline P(reopen 30d) = ~15% has held for 11 days because nothing has happened to move it.

This rule is appropriate for most classes of new information. A diplomatic announcement, a naval incident, a carrier withdrawal, or a confirmed back-channel are discrete events that arrive with identifiable probability mass. The no-decay rule prevents the tracker from drifting on noise or manufacturing signal from silence.

But calendar compression is not noise. It is a structural feature of the partial arrangement scenario that has a measurable, time-dependent feasibility constraint. And 11 days of silence is the mechanism by which that constraint tightens.

Anatomy of the Partial Arrangement Scenario

The partial arrangement scenario carries 15% probability and contributes 9 of the 15 percentage points to P(reopen). It posits a memorandum or traffic agreement — short of a comprehensive peace deal — that restores commercial vessel transits to something approaching pre-war levels. Iran gets partial export revenue relief; the US gets a visible de-escalation deliverable without conceding the blockade framework.

What Calendar Compression Means for the Scenario Probability

The partial arrangement scenario is assigned 15% probability. That probability reflects the assessed likelihood that this scenario resolves within the window — it is a joint probability of “a partial arrangement becomes politically possible” AND “it executes fast enough to open Hormuz within 30 days.”

On April 28 (the last day the tracker explicitly updated), the window had 32 days remaining. Execution was feasible across the full plausible range. Today the window has 30 days remaining — still feasible, but compressing. The underlying 15% was calibrated for a window where a Round 3 announcement could come at any point.

Now consider what happens if no announcement comes in the next 11 days. On May 20, the viable execution window shrinks to 19 days — feasible only at minimum viable pace, with zero slack for political hesitation, procedural delay, or draft disputes. The “political possibility” half of the partial arrangement scenario is unchanged. The “executes fast enough” half is eroding.

Analytical judgment: If no Round 3 date is announced by May 20, the partial arrangement scenario’s effective contribution to P(reopen) should compress from ~9 pp (15% scenario × 60% open rate) toward ~4–6 pp — consistent with a scenario probability of 8–10% rather than 15%. This would trim the headline P(reopen) from ~15% to ~12–13%. The tracker has not yet made this adjustment because no explicit signal has arrived; May 20 is the threshold at which calendar compression becomes the signal.

Why the Tracker Hasn’t Moved Yet

The no-decay rule is not wrong. It is calibrated for situations where the window is large and scenario feasibility is not yet materially constrained by time. A partial arrangement announced on May 9 with 30 days of window is not structurally different from one announced on May 1 with 38 days — the execution challenge is minor at the margin. The tracker correctly held through April and the first week of May.

The reason May 20 is the right threshold — rather than today or some earlier date — is that probability revisions are only defensible when the constraint is materially binding. Twelve days from now, if no Round 3 date exists, the execution window will have closed enough that the scenario probability is genuinely lower, not just notionally tighter. That’s a qualitative change, not just a marginal one.

There is also an asymmetry to consider: a Round 3 announcement tomorrow would be an explicit positive signal (+5–10% per the watch list) and would trigger an upward revision regardless of calendar position. The absence of that announcement accumulates quietly. The tracker’s methodology is faster on positive signals than on structural erosion — a design choice appropriate for discrete events but worth flagging for time-bounded scenario feasibility.

The 23% Rupture Scenario Is Unaffected

Calendar compression affects only the partial arrangement scenario (and marginally the comprehensive deal scenario, which was already low-probability). The 23% rupture scenario does not have a similar time dependency — war can restart at any point. The 55% stalemate scenario is explicitly time-neutral by definition. So the headline probability structure shifts in one direction: if calendar erosion becomes a signal, P(stalemate) increases roughly proportionally to the P(partial arrangement) reduction, while the rupture and comprehensive deal probabilities hold.

The rupture scenario’s 23% has been stable since late April. It is not building in probability in the current data, but it remains the most consequential downside tail: a rupture would collapse P(reopen) by 13–15 pp immediately. No new tripwires have fired since the Kuwait proxy drone strike on April 24 — the most recent kinetic incident. The IRGC has not conducted new naval seizures since April 22 (17 days ago). The rupture risk is ambient, not escalating.

Bottom Line

P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, unchanged. The 11-day static run is not analytically empty — it is compressing the partial arrangement scenario’s execution window one day at a time. At the current pace, the tracker’s first downward revision since the ceasefire becomes analytically defensible around May 20, when a Round 3 announcement would leave fewer than 20 days to execute — marginal feasibility only. Before then, the probability is correctly held: the window is still open, the signal is still absent, and the stalemate is still winning.

The asymmetry to watch: a Round 3 announcement tomorrow triggers an immediate upward revision. Continued silence through May 20 triggers a modest downward one. After 11 days of identical data, the next 11 days carry more probability mass than any equivalent period since April 22.
