The IRGC Naval Channel as Primary Discriminator

Yesterday flagged the IRGC Navy’s 26-day post-seizure silence as the single most informative sub-indicator inside the broader 21-day pre-cliff stasis. Today operationalizes that flag. The IRGC Naval channel has now been silent for 27 days since the Apr 22 seizures: no further seizures, no escalation against the held vessels, no public quid pro quo demand, no diplomatic deployment of the vessels as a bargaining chit. That is 27 consecutive days of disciplined restraint by an instrument that has demonstrated both the capability and the operational willingness to act.

Rule C’s mechanism — visible diplomatic closure raises the marginal attractiveness of escalation — predicts that the discipline was contingent on the partial-arrangement scenario’s pendency, not on an underlying decision to refrain. If the discipline survives May 20 and the IRGC Navy stays silent through May 24, Rule C is falsified more strongly than the headline daily-rate analysis suggests. Conversely, a fresh IRGC vessel seizure in the May 21–24 window is much stronger evidence for Rule C than any other event class on the watch list.

Pre-publishing the IRGC Naval channel as the primary discriminator means: a single fresh seizure in the 72-hour post-cliff window will be read as Rule C corroboration even if other watch-list channels remain quiet. Conversely, a quiet IRGC Naval channel through May 24 will be read as Rule C falsification even if some other watch-list channel produces noise that could otherwise be attributed to escalation. This is the rule-ranking discipline the natural-experiment framing requires.

The 21-Day Stasis is Bayesian Evidence Against Rule C

The pre-cliff stasis now reaches 21 consecutive days. The methodology priced eight watch-list indicators with a combined pre-filter unconditional daily trigger rate of ~2.5%. The unconditional joint probability of any watch-list firing over 21 days at those base rates is roughly 1 − (1 − 0.025)21 ≈ 41%. The fact that zero firings have actually been observed — the bottom ~59% of the predicted distribution — is moderate Bayesian evidence that the underlying daily firing rates are lower than the methodology has priced.

Translating that into rule-specific implications: Rule B (empty 96 hours post-cliff, no watch-list firings) gets the most direct corroboration from the 21-day stasis. The world has just demonstrated that 21-day stretches of nothing-happens are physically realizable; an additional 96 hours of nothing-happens immediately afterward is not a stretch. Rule C (naval-escalation cluster within 72 hours of the cliff) faces a higher empirical hurdle: it has to explain why the same naval channel that was silent for 27 days suddenly activates in the 72 hours after the cliff fires. Rule A (neutral anchor revision) sits closest to neutral — the 21-day silence is consistent with the existing reporting cadence and a routine post-cliff update is consistent with that cadence continuing.

The Bayesian update from the stasis is therefore mildly toward Rule B and mildly against Rule C. The methodology’s pre-committed primary read remains Rule C on theoretical grounds (the visible-diplomatic-closure mechanism), but the pre-cliff evidence is empirically pressured against it. This pre-publication explicitly preserves the right to revise downward to Rule A or Rule B if the post-cliff stream is consistent with the empirical pressure rather than the theoretical mechanism.

What the Last 24 Hours Cannot Change

The May 18 story priced the credibility-filtered probability of a cliff avert at ~1% over the remaining 48 hours. At T−24, that probability halves on a flat-prior decay to ~0.5%. To avert the cliff in the next 24 hours, a Round 3 announcement would have to (i) be issued, (ii) name a date, (iii) name a host, (iv) name an Iranian delegation lead, (v) name a US-side participation level, and (vi) fall within the central-case 22-day execution stack — all within a single news cycle. The base rate for that compound event is essentially zero; the cliff fires May 20 with ~99.5% structural certainty.

The methodology’s remaining work in the next 24 hours is therefore not waiting for an avert that will not arrive. It is two things: (i) confirming that this pre-publication is dated and timestamped before 00:00 UTC May 20 (this story is the artifact of that confirmation), and (ii) updating source/hormuz-state.md and the dashboard timeline so that the three-rule ladder is visible on the live dashboard before the cliff fires. Both have been completed today; the methodology is now in the state the May 17 and May 18 stories said it had to reach before the cliff.

The Tracker A Implication, Pre-Published

The reconstitution plateau is now Day 26. The cliff is a Tracker B event but its rule choice changes the conditional probability that Tracker A becomes operationally live again.

Under the pre-published Rule C posterior (P(rupture-scenario) = 28%), Tracker A is analytically near-live in the rolling window: the conditional probability of resumption is meaningful enough that the reconstitution plateau and the underground launcher reserve become operational inputs to current decisions, not just paused observations. Under the pre-published Rule B posterior (P(rupture-scenario) = 23%), Tracker A remains a paused reference; the Apr 19 ISW baseline is the authoritative read. Under Rule A (27%), Tracker A sits between — a tail-risk input but not an active probability flow. The May 21–24 natural experiment therefore also determines, pre-published under timestamp today, how seriously the frozen depletion data should be treated going forward.
