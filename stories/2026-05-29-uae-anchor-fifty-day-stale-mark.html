UAE Anchor 50-Day Stale Mark: The First Measurement-Quality Landmark of the Post-Horizon Regime

May 29, 2026 — Day 52 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz Measurement quality
At 00:00 UTC May 29, the UAE official cumulative anchor (Apr 9) crossed the 50-day stale mark. This is the first measurement-quality landmark of the post-horizon regime and the first day on which both authoritative cumulative anchors (ISW/FDD/LWJ Apr 19 ballistic-posture read at 40 days stale; UAE Apr 9 cumulative tally at 50 days stale) sit simultaneously beyond their respective half-quarter freshness thresholds. The headline P(Hormuz reopen 30d) does not move — ~6.3% (Rule B, single live ladder) for the tenth consecutive day; ninth consecutive null calendar day since the cliff. The 50-day mark is structurally distinct from the methodology-quality landmarks (cliff May 20, revision May 25, comparator drop May 28) that occupied the May 20–28 sequence: it does not move the dial, the four-indicator watch list, the single-rule ladder, or the 30-day rolling window. But it does change the marginal informativeness of any subsequent UAE anchor refresh, and the separation between measurement-quality and methodology-quality landmarks was a design property of the post-cliff regime — today is the first calendar evidence the separation holds in operation.

Two Kinds of Landmarks

The May 20–28 sequence was unusually dense with structural events. Four scheduled dashboard transitions fired on calendar thresholds inside nine days: the partial-arrangement scenario eviction (May 20), the methodology revision from Rule C to Rule B (May 25), the reconvergence horizon comparator drop (May 28), and the in-between four-day natural-experiment window (May 21–24). Each of these was a methodology-quality landmark: each had a pre-committed rule, a pre-published expected outcome, a discrete dashboard change at the calendar trigger, and a published purpose that was internal to the methodology’s subjective Bayesian architecture.

Today is a different kind of landmark. The UAE anchor 50-day stale mark is a measurement-quality landmark. It does not derive from a methodology rule. It does not change the scenario ladder, the watch list, the headline, or the rolling window. It is a property of the underlying data infrastructure: at 00:00 UTC May 29, the dashboard’s authoritative cumulative source-of-record for the Gulf theater (the UAE Ministry of Defence’s Apr 9 release) has gone unrefreshed for 50 calendar days. The number itself — UAE BM 537, CM 26, drones 2,221 — remains the most authoritative published cumulative on the dashboard, and the methodology continues to anchor its derived estimates against it. But the freshness of that anchor has now compounded into a separate analytical object that the dashboard has to publish through.

The post-cliff regime was structured to handle these two kinds of landmarks separately. Methodology-quality landmarks change the published dashboard architecture; measurement-quality landmarks change a footnote. Today is the first day this separation has been tested live in the post-horizon regime, and the test passes: the four-scenario architecture, the four-indicator watch list, the single-rule ladder, and the 30-day rolling window all carry through May 29 unchanged.

Both Anchors Stale, Simultaneously, for the First Time

The dashboard’s two authoritative anchors come from different reporting cadences. The UAE MoD release is the primary cumulative tally — updated roughly every 8–12 days during the war’s reporting-fresh period, with each release moving the BM/CM/drone counters and feeding directly into the depletion-tracker chart. The ISW/FDD/LWJ posture series is the primary qualitative posture anchor — updated roughly every 10–15 days during the war, with each release moving the launcher count and the “remaining stockpile fraction” estimate (~70% BMs, ~40% drones, ~60% launchers as of the Apr 19 read).

Both anchors went into the ceasefire fresh: UAE refreshed Apr 9, ISW refreshed Apr 19, with both falling inside the ceasefire’s opening fortnight. Through the war’s active-fire phase, the typical refresh cadence held a freshness window of under 15 days. With direct fire stopped on Apr 11 and no new operational developments to motivate a fresh authoritative refresh, both series have drifted past their reporting cadences and into a stale zone the dashboard has not previously had to publish through.

Why the Refresh Cadence Decoupled from the Cease-fire

Reporting cadence is not the same thing as event cadence. The UAE MoD and ISW/FDD/LWJ refresh series during active fire were driven by two motivating forces: new salvos to count (UAE’s charter) and new posture revisions to assess (ISW’s charter). The ceasefire severed both. With Iran’s direct-fire interval at zero since Apr 11, the UAE’s cumulative counter has no new fire events to roll up — the Apr 9 numbers are the cumulative through the end of active fire, and there is no analytical pressure to publish a new tally with the same values plus zero. ISW’s posture series faces a parallel constraint: with no fresh combat to assess and the reconstitution plateau at Day 36 by an authoritative count taken on Day 19, the published estimate would not move unless ISW judged that underground reserves had been tapped or new platforms had been observed.

The result is that the ceasefire structurally produces anchor-staleness. The dashboard’s authoritative reporting infrastructure was tuned for a high-event-rate environment; the post-ceasefire low-event-rate environment is exactly the one in which both anchors will drift past freshness thresholds without anyone exercising operator discretion. This was foreseeable as soon as the indefinite extension was announced on Apr 21, and the methodology page accommodates it: the post-cliff regime’s separation between measurement-quality and methodology-quality landmarks exists precisely so that the dashboard can publish through anchor staleness without the perception that the underlying Bayesian read is degraded.

What Changes at the 50-Day Mark (and What Does Not)

The substantive consequence of crossing 50 days is not in the published dashboard. It is in the procedure for handling a fresh UAE refresh, should one arrive. During the war-active period (refresh cadence ~8–12 days), a new UAE tally landed against a freshness window inside which the cumulative counter could be auto-swapped: the new release replaces the old anchor, the depletion chart redraws to the new endpoint, and the methodology page documents the swap as a routine update. The cumulative drift between the old and new releases was bounded by the inter-release interval, and the diff was interpretable as “new events counted between release X and release Y.”

Past the 50-day stale mark, a fresh UAE refresh lands against 50 days of compounded counter-of-record drift. The diff is no longer interpretable as “new events counted” — it is a mix of (a) any genuinely new events that occurred in the ceasefire period (mostly Iraq-launched proxy events, possibly a few minor incidents), (b) reclassification of pre-ceasefire events under updated source attribution, and (c) reporting-method corrections to the Apr 9 baseline itself. The diff sources are no longer separable in the published cumulative. A fresh tally landing today is therefore a candidate for a methodology-page footnote documenting which components of the diff the methodology attributes to which source, rather than the automatic anchor swap-in available during the reporting-fresh period.

The Hormuz scenario ladder is structurally insulated from the cumulative anchor: the ladder is built on scenario probabilities and P(open | scenario) values that derive from political and military judgment, not from the counter-of-record. So even a substantial UAE refresh would not move the headline. The depletion chart, by contrast, is built on the cumulative, and its endpoint depends on the anchor refresh procedure. A 50-day-stale UAE refresh is therefore a structural risk for the depletion chart but not for the Hormuz ladder — precisely the kind of asymmetric measurement-side risk the dual-tracker architecture was designed to keep separable.

The Pre-Committed Separation

The post-cliff regime’s pre-committed treatment of measurement-quality landmarks was published in two places. The first is the methodology page’s source-hierarchy section, which ranks the UAE MoD as anchor #1 but flags reporting-cadence drift as a known structural risk — the relevant clause is that anchor swaps inside the reporting-fresh window are automatic, while anchor swaps in a stale-zone refresh are flagged as “requires attribution footnote.” The second is the post-eviction natural-experiment rule itself, which explicitly distinguished methodology landmarks (calendar thresholds with pre-committed dashboard changes) from measurement landmarks (anchor refreshes with footnote-only treatment).

The separation was not invented today. It was pre-committed during the May 14–19 baseline lock-in window, when the methodology team was working through what the post-cliff dashboard’s steady-state response to authoritative-source drift should look like. The May 28 reconvergence horizon’s rationale page already flagged the 50-day UAE mark as “the next dashboard observable on the calendar — measurement-quality landmark, not methodology landmark.” Today’s publication therefore lands against an already-published expected behavior. The test is whether the dashboard actually executes that behavior in operation, and it does: the headline ~6.3% does not move, the scenario ladder does not move, the watch list does not move, the rolling window advances by one day as on every other publication day.

A Concrete Counterfactual

To make the asymmetry concrete: suppose the UAE MoD published a fresh cumulative refresh today, reporting BM 549 (+12), CM 28 (+2), drones 2,261 (+40) versus the Apr 9 baseline. What would the dashboard do under the pre-committed footnote procedure?

  • Depletion chart: a methodology-page footnote documenting that of the (+12, +2, +40) diff, roughly (+8, +1, +30) is attributable to Iraq-launched proxy events (notably the Apr 24 Kuwait incident and the Apr 8 post-ceasefire salvos), with the residual (+4, +1, +10) flagged as reporting-method correction. Chart endpoint then conditionally redrawn against the attributed cumulative; old endpoint preserved in the source files for audit.
  • Hormuz scenario ladder: no change. The ladder is built on scenario probabilities, not on the cumulative; the headline ~6.3% does not move.
  • Tracker A conditional liveness: no change. The 23% figure is Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario), not a cumulative-derived value.
  • Watch list: no change. None of the four operative indicators is anchored on the UAE cumulative.
  • Reconstitution plateau Day count: conditional. If the UAE refresh includes an updated launcher posture (it normally does not — ISW carries launcher counts), the plateau Day count would reset; otherwise unchanged at Day 36.

This is the structural payoff of the dual-tracker separation: the measurement-side risk concentrates almost entirely on the depletion chart, and the Hormuz tracker continues to operate from its political-judgment scenario ladder without contamination. The post-horizon regime’s single-rule architecture compounds the insulation: there is no rule-weighting machinery for a stale anchor refresh to perturb.

The Tracker A Boundary, Day 5

Today is also the fifth full calendar day on which Iran’s fire-silence interval is longer than the active-fire phase that preceded it (Day 48 silence vs Day 43 active fire = 112% of pre-silence duration). The interval has now compounded to roughly 12% above the boundary — small in absolute terms, but the cumulative trajectory continues to read as Iran’s revealed strategic commitment under the ceasefire architecture rather than as a tactical pause.

The frozen tracker’s depletion math does not move at the 50-day mark for the same reason it did not move at the horizon: the math is paused, not retired. Iran still retains ~70% of the pre-war BM stockpile and ~40% of the pre-war drone stockpile per the (now 40-day-stale) Apr 19 ISW read; the effective launcher count holds at ~200 per the same baseline; the days-to-functional-zero figure sits at ~100 from any future resumption. The 40-day ISW stale mark crossed today applies a methodology-page footnote to those values, not a dashboard transition. The chart remains at its last live state; all data is intact and resumes automatically if hostilities restart.

What Comes Next

The post-horizon dashboard now has no methodology landmarks on its forward calendar. There is no scheduled cliff, no scheduled revision, no scheduled horizon, no scheduled comparator drop. Under Rule B’s implied per-day P(null) ~0.93, the modal evolution from here is an extended quiet stretch — a two-week stretch without any watch-list event has joint probability ~38%, a four-week stretch ~13%, a six-week stretch ~5%. The base-rate expectation is that the dashboard reads ~6.3% / 70/23/7/0 / 23% Tracker A conditional liveness for an extended period punctuated only by watch-list events.

Measurement-quality landmarks do remain on the forward calendar. The next is the ISW Apr 19 anchor crossing its 50-day stale mark on June 8 — at which point both anchors will be in the same stale band rather than ten days apart, and the methodology page will publish a second-anchor footnote covering the ISW series under the same procedure that today applies to UAE. After June 8, the dashboard’s expected next non-base-rate observable is whichever comes first: a fresh authoritative refresh (footnote-handled), a watch-list indicator firing (impact-calibrated handling), or the published rolling window’s Jun 28 endpoint approaching without resolution (no special handling — the window simply advances).
