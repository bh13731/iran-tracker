What Flips at 00:00 UTC June 8 on the Depletion Side

The procedural flip is precise. Inside the freshness window (anchor age ≤ 50 days), a fresh authoritative ISW refresh produces an automatic anchor swap-in: the depletion chart endpoint redraws to the new value, the cumulative counter updates to the new total, and the methodology page documents the swap as routine. Past 00:00 UTC June 8 (anchor age > 50 days), the same refresh produces a methodology-page footnote-with-decomposition: the refresh must be decomposed into three components — (a) genuinely new events since the last anchor, (b) reclassification of prior-anchor data, and (c) reporting-method correction — with chart redraw conditional on (a) being non-zero and the cumulative counter not auto-swapping.

The decomposition components differ structurally from the May 29 UAE crossing, even though both procedural flips fire under the same methodology rule. The UAE Apr 9 anchor is a cumulative tally; its decomposition components are event-side (events missed in the prior tally vs reclassified events vs reporting-rule corrections). The ISW Apr 19 anchor is a posture estimate; its decomposition components are methodology-side (events since the anchor date vs underlying counting-rule revisions vs source-mix changes). The two anchor types decompose differently because they answer different questions: the UAE tally asks “how many things have we counted,” the ISW posture asks “how many things does Iran have.” The procedural-flip rule fires identically on both, but the post-flip decomposition the methodology page produces is structurally distinct.

Why the depletion-side flip matters more than the Hormuz-side flip did. The May 29 UAE flip applied to a cumulative anchor that feeds the depletion-side charts but not the Hormuz scenario ladder. The June 8 ISW flip applies to a posture anchor that feeds the depletion-side launcher chart (the binding constraint on Iran’s ballistic-missile effectiveness, per the methodology’s core rules) and is the same anchor that produced the ~200-launcher reconstitution-plateau floor — the most consequential single number the depletion-side tracker has published since the cease-fire began. A fresh ISW refresh today would update the chart endpoint and the counter automatically; a fresh ISW refresh on Jun 9 would require the methodology page to decompose the diff before either the chart or the counter moves. The 24-hour window matters because the cost of the same refresh changes inside it.

What the Asymmetric Surprise Budget Costs on Day Two

Yesterday the calibration baseline crossed sub-50% for the first time: 0.9310 ≈ 48%. Today it falls one further factor of 0.93 to 45%. The marginal calibration cost of each additional null day, in baseline terms, is approximately −3 pp per day — the compounding rate is invariant under the regime, but the operationally-relevant quantity is how much surprise budget the marginal day consumes when the headline does not move.

Through May 28–Jun 5 (Days 1–9 post-horizon) the calibration baseline was always more likely than not: every additional null day was the modal case under the regime’s own calibration, and the surprise budget was carried symmetrically (the burden was on a watch-list event firing, not on a null day persisting). Through Jun 6 onward (Day 10+) the baseline is less likely than not: every additional null day is now the modestly-surprising case, and the surprise budget is carried asymmetrically (a watch-list event firing is the modal case, a null day persisting is the surprise). The dial has not moved, the ladder has not moved, the watch list has not moved — what has moved is the regime’s own answer to the question “is the trajectory I have observed more or less likely than not under my calibration baseline?”

Day 2 of the asymmetric surprise budget is structurally interesting because it is the first day on which the regime’s self-reported surprise is compounding rather than newly opened. Yesterday the regime was obligated to publish under the budget for the first time; today the regime is obligated to publish under a budget that has tightened by an additional 3 pp. The compounding mechanism is invariant; what changes calendar day to calendar day is the depth of the asymmetry. By June 10 (Day 14, end of the second weekly cycle) the baseline falls to 0.9314 ≈ 37%; by June 17 (Day 21, end of the third weekly cycle) it falls to 0.9321 ≈ 22%. The regime continues to publish through each subsequent crossing under the pre-committed separation rule.

What the Eve Is and Isn’t

Today’s eve is not itself a landmark. No architectural transition fires at 00:00 UTC June 7; no procedural-flip activates; no calibration baseline crosses a round-number threshold; no dial moves. The eve is a calendar position relative to a landmark that fires tomorrow — the same kind of position the May 19 entry held relative to the May 20 cliff and the May 27 entry held relative to the May 28 horizon. What the eve does carry is the methodology’s pre-publication obligation: with 24 hours remaining to a calendar-fixed procedural transition, the rationale page is using the window to lock the post-transition behaviour against a hard pre-dated baseline. Tomorrow the procedural flip lands against today’s pre-publication; the methodology page’s description of the post-flip procedure is published before the procedure fires, not after.

The eve also is not a signal to revise the rule. Rule B remains the single live ladder; the four-scenario architecture remains unchanged; the four-indicator watch list remains unchanged; the 30-day rolling window shifts forward by one day to Jun 7–Jul 7. The reversal procedure remains as defined: a Rule B-falsifying event would require a separately-published methodology-page reversal; in the absence of such an event, the rule stands across the procedural flip. The procedural-flip mechanism the rationale page publishes tomorrow is not a rule reversal; it is a refresh-handling procedure change that fires on a calendar clock irrespective of whether a refresh arrives.

What the eve is, structurally, is the third instance of the regime’s published “T−24h to a calendar-fixed procedural transition” pattern. The May 19 eve preceded a Hormuz-side methodology-quality landmark with a dial move. The May 27 eve preceded a Hormuz-side methodology-quality landmark without a dial move. Today’s eve precedes a depletion-side measurement-quality landmark co-located with a rationale-page calibration-quality landmark, both pre-committed to be dial-invariant. The three eves trace the regime’s evolution from architectural events that move the dial, through architectural events that change the rationale page without moving the dial, to architectural events that fire on the depletion side of the dual-tracker architecture without moving anything on the Hormuz side. The regime publishes through each pattern at the discipline level it pre-committed to; the dial remains insulated; the methodology page tightens.
