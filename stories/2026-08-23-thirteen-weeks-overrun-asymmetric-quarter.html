Thirteen Weeks of Overrun: The Fire-Silence Clock Completes a Quarter Beyond the War

August 23, 2026 — Day 138 Analysis Cease-fire Fire-silence Self-normalizing metrics Calibration
At 00:00 UTC the overrun — direct Iran fire-silence measured against the war’s own 43-day active-fire interval rather than against the calendar — reaches exactly 91 days: thirteen seven-day blocks, a full quarter on the asymmetric side of the boundary. Total silence is 134 days = 311.6% of the interval it is measured against, and the overrun by itself now exceeds twice that interval. It is the largest compositional landmark the tracker carries. P(Hormuz reopen 30d) does not move, and the reason it does not move is the most instructive thing on the dashboard.

The One Clock That Doesn’t Drift

The dashboard carries eight running counters. Seven of them — war day, cease-fire day, post-horizon day, IRGC naval day, reconstitution-plateau day, and the two anchor-staleness clocks — are calendar counters. They share a defect: they have no natural scale. “Cease-fire Day 138” is a bigger number than “Cease-fire Day 80” but there is no non-arbitrary sense in which it is a different kind of observation. Every day makes the number larger, which is exactly why round-number crossings on those clocks are published as landmarks and then explicitly discounted.

The overrun is the exception. It is defined as

overrun = days of direct fire-silence − 43, where 43 days is the war’s pre-silence active-fire interval (Feb 17 – Apr 11) — the span over which Iran actually fired.

That subtraction is what makes it self-normalizing. The overrun does not ask how long the truce has lasted; it asks how long Iran has declined to fire relative to how long Iran fired. A given value means the same thing in month one and month five, because the yardstick is fixed by the war’s own behaviour rather than by the calendar. When the overrun was 7 days (May 31), the honest reading was “a long pause inside a war.” At 91 days it is a different observation in kind, not merely in magnitude.

Two things are worth pulling out of that table. First, today’s 91 days is exactly 13 × 7 — a full quarter measured in weeks, on a clock whose unit is the war’s own tempo. Second, and more consequential: on August 18 the overrun passed 86 days, which is twice the reference interval. The asymmetric side is no longer a margin on the war. It is larger than the war it is measured against, and by today it is larger by more than a full extra fire-campaign’s worth of days.

The compression that makes this readable. Iran fought its direct-fire campaign in 43 days and has now declined to fire for 134. If the firing campaign were a working quarter, the silence would be three of them. Whatever the ceasefire is, it is not a pause in a tempo — the tempo it would be a pause in accounts for less than a quarter of the elapsed conflict.

What It Changes: Composition, Not Level

Rule B — the single live ladder since the May 28 reconvergence horizon — reads Stalemate 70 / Rupture 23 / Comprehensive 7 / Partial 0. The overrun bears on the first two weights and on neither’s contribution to the dial.

On the Stalemate side the effect is to remove a reading. At an overrun of a week or two, 70% could be justified by “Iran is regrouping and will resume when ready” — a tactical account, which implies the weight is temporary and should decay as reconstitution completes. At thirteen weeks of overrun, with the reconstitution plateau itself at Day 122 and the launcher floor read as materially better than the April baseline, that account no longer survives contact with the calendar. Iran has had the capacity to resume for months and has not. The 70% now rests on revealed preference, which is a more stable footing than the tactical account it replaced.

On the Rupture side the effect is a ceiling. The 23% weight is priced against a base rate of ceasefire collapse, and the primary falsification channel — a fresh IRGC naval incident — has now been silent for Day 123, roughly 17.6 weeks. That is beyond every historical IRGC seizure resolution band the tracker benchmarks against (Stena Impero ~10 weeks, MSC Aries ~6, Niovi ~6, Korean-flagged ~13). Two vessels remain held, unused and un-negotiated. The overrun and the naval silence are independent channels pointing the same way: rupture is not getting more likely with time.

Why a Landmark This Large Still Moves Nothing

Here is the structural point, and it is the one that keeps this tracker honest. Both of the scenarios the overrun informs carry P(open | scenario) = 0. A ceasefire that holds keeps the US blockade in place, because the blockade was tied by declaration to a comprehensive peace deal and not to the ceasefire. A ceasefire that ruptures obviously does not open the Strait. The entire published probability runs through one channel:

7% × 90% = 6.3%, and nothing else. With Partial evicted at the May 20 cliff, the Comprehensive scenario is the sole positive-side path. Redistributing weight between Stalemate and Rupture — which is all the overrun can do — moves probability between two zeros.

So the overrun is one-directional with respect to the dial. Growth in it can only make the blockade read more durable, never less. That asymmetry is not a bug in the presentation; it is the honest shape of the situation. The single most impressive fact on the dashboard — that Iran has held fire for more than three times as long as it fought — is, for the question this tracker actually asks, evidence against reopening. P(reopen) holds at ~6.3% for the ninety-sixth consecutive day.

The Calibration Channel, Still Refusing to Learn

The one number that does move every day is the calibration baseline: under Rule B’s fixed per-day probability of a null day of ~0.93, the joint probability of the observed streak. At post-horizon Day 88 that is 0.9388 ≈ 0.17% — roughly one in 590. It crossed single digits on June 28, closed a weekly cycle in single digits on July 1, and passed sub-1% on July 30.

A one-in-590 read is real evidence that 0.93 was too pessimistic about the daily event rate. The methodology still declines to act on it, for the same reason it declined at 48%, at 22% and at 0.96%: a single uninterrupted ceasefire is one realization. Streak length and the parameter that generated it are not separately identified from one draw, and re-estimating on the streak would be fitting the parameter to the only data the parameter produced. The pre-commitment holds 0.93 fixed.

And the pre-commitment costs almost nothing, which is why it is easy to keep. Even if the null rate were re-estimated upward, the marginal weight would accrue to Stalemate — P(open) = 0 — and be drawn only thinly from the 7% Comprehensive channel. The dial would move a fraction of a point, well inside the ±8 pp band the methodology already declares.

The Frozen Side, Reading the Same Clock

The overrun is unusual in belonging to both trackers at once. On the Hormuz side it is a compositional input to the scenario weights. On the depletion side it is the frozen tracker’s own measure of how long it has had nothing to record: 134 days in which no missile, drone or launcher figure has needed revision, against a 43-day campaign that generated the entire series. The paused state has now run 3.12× as long as the state it paused.

The reconstitution plateau sits at Day 122 against a single authoritative launcher read (ISW, Apr 19) now 126 days stale — so the plateau and the staleness are the same flat line. That confound is real but one-sided: maintenance attrition is slow and a dig-out programme only adds, so the uncertainty lives entirely in the upper tail. The ~200-launcher floor is robust to it, and the floor is the only quantity the frozen tracker’s live output depends on. Conditional liveness stays locked at ~23% — Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario), unchanged for ninety-six days.

The Clean Statement

Thirteen weeks of overrun is the strongest posture reading the tracker has ever produced, and it is worth exactly zero percentage points. The silence is 311.6% of the war it interrupted; the asymmetric side alone is more than twice the firing campaign; the naval falsification channel has been quiet longer than any comparable episode on record. All of it lands on two scenarios that cannot open the Strait. The blockade was decoupled from the ceasefire by declaration in April, and one hundred and thirty-four days of silence have now confirmed the decoupling about as thoroughly as a single case can. P(Hormuz reopen 30d) = ~6.3%, ninety-sixth consecutive day — 7% × 90%, through one channel, and no other.
