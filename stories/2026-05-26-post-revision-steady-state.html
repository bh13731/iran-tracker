Post-Revision Steady State: What Rule B Looks Like 48 Hours In

May 26, 2026 — Day 49 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
The mechanical methodology revision from Rule C (smoothed asymmetric) to Rule B (all-into-stalemate) fired on schedule at 00:00 UTC May 25, confirming the four-day natural-experiment outcome and locking the new primary read across five published values without operator discretion. Today is post-revision Day 2; the first 48 hours of the new regime have produced the same observed signal as the four pre-revision natural-experiment days: zero events across all seven enumerated read-grid channels. Six compounded null days since the May 20 cliff shift rule posterior weights from the cliff-day uniform 33/33/33 to roughly ~10/30/60 in favor of Rule B — the rule is now firmly dominant with ~60% of the remaining hypothesis mass. The headline dashboard read of ~6.3% is unchanged for the seventh consecutive day: the cliff (May 20) and the revision (May 25) have both passed without the dial moving, and the rule-invariant headline structure built into the May 19 baseline lock-in pre-publication has now carried both endpoints. This story is not about a forecast change — there isn’t one. It is about what the post-revision regime actually looks like in operation, what would now move the dial from here, and what the two-endpoint pre-publication test tells us about whether the methodology is doing what it was designed to do.

The IRGC Channel Re-Tagging

The methodology revision did not change the four-indicator post-cliff watch list. Each entry remains on the dashboard with approximately its pre-revision calibrated probability impact. What changed at midnight was the epistemic interpretation of the primary discriminator. Under Rule C (smoothed asymmetric, primary through 23:59 UTC May 24), a fresh IRGC seizure would have been a corroboration event: a naval cluster after a calendar-threshold eviction is the exact signature the rupture-weighted redistribution was published to anticipate. Under Rule B (all-into-stalemate, primary from 00:00 UTC May 25), the same event is now a falsification event: the all-into-stalemate redistribution’s implied prior is that the post-cliff regime is a continuation of the pre-cliff equilibrium, and a fresh seizure breaks that equilibrium in a way that re-elevates P(rupture-scenario) and partially re-opens the Rule C posterior.

The same indicator carries the same calibrated probability impact under both rules; the only thing that changes is whether the indicator’s realization would push the posterior toward or away from the primary read. The methodology pre-committed to this re-tagging language on May 18–19 precisely because the post-revision watch list would otherwise inherit a stale interpretation of its primary discriminator. The re-framing is therefore a published consequence of the rule flip, not an after-the-fact re-rationalization.

The operational consequence of the inversion is asymmetric. Under Rule C primary, a continued IRGC silence was the surprising outcome (Rule C’s implied per-day P(null) was ~0.70, so four consecutive null days had a joint probability of ~24%). Under Rule B primary, a continued IRGC silence is the predicted outcome (Rule B’s implied per-day P(null) is ~0.93, so each additional null day buys correspondingly less posterior movement). The natural-experiment window’s discriminating power is largely consumed; under Rule B primary the marginal information value of an additional null day is roughly 5–7% of the cliff-day marginal. The watch-list base rates have reasserted.

What Would Now Move the Dial

With Rule B primary, the asymmetric upside / downside picture under the rolling 30-day window is sharply skewed. The headline ~6.3% is composed of:

  • Stalemate (70%, P(open) = 0%) → 0 pp contribution. The modal scenario contributes nothing to P(reopen) by construction; the blockade-ceasefire decoupling is now baked into the dominant scenario. A continuation of the stalemate regime simply confirms the published read; only a regime change moves the dial.
  • Rupture (23%, P(open) = 0%) → 0 pp contribution. The rupture scenario also contributes nothing to P(reopen) under any plausible reading — if direct fire resumes, the US blockade tightens rather than lifts. The 23 pp of rupture probability is a tail risk that lives entirely on the Tracker A side of the dashboard; on the Hormuz side, rupture is a 0 pp contributor.
  • Comprehensive deal (7%, P(open) = 90%) → 6.3 pp contribution. The entire ~6.3% headline P(reopen) flows through a single scenario whose probability the watch list has been static against for 49 days. The headline is now structurally dependent on a single narrow channel.
  • Partial (0%, evicted May 20) → 0 pp. The 9 pp this scenario contributed under the pre-cliff ladder evicted mechanically and cannot come back inside the current rolling window.

The implication is that the dial is now harder to move in either direction than it was at the cliff. To push the dial materially up, the watch list needs either (i) a Comprehensive deal signal that moves P(scenario) for the 7% bucket toward 10–15%, or (ii) a commercial-transit cluster substantial enough to revise P(open | Stalemate) above 0% — both of which require named-source authoritative reporting that has been absent for 47+ days. To push the dial materially down, the watch list needs either (iii) a fresh IRGC seizure cluster that re-elevates P(rupture-scenario) and partially re-opens Rule C, or (iv) outright ceasefire rupture that makes Tracker A live and collapses the Hormuz tracker entirely — both of which have been absent for 34+ days and 45+ days respectively. The two-sided absence is structural, not coincidental: the entire post-cliff watch list is observing a single equilibrium that has been stationary across cliff and revision.

The Tracker A Boundary, Day 2

Yesterday (May 25) was the first calendar day on which Iran’s fire-silence interval was longer than the active-fire phase that preceded it (Day 44 silence vs Day 43 active fire = 102% of pre-silence duration). Today is the second full calendar day on the asymmetric side of that boundary (Day 45 = 105% of pre-silence duration). This is not a methodology threshold — nothing in the dashboard changes mechanically at the crossing — but it is a calendar-coincident landmark that lands within 48 hours of the methodology revision and that has interpretive weight for the conditional liveness framing.

Under Rule B primary the conditional liveness of Tracker A over the rolling 30-day window is ~23%, down from Rule C’s ~28% at the cliff baseline. The 5 pp drop is now reinforced by a calendar fact that the methodology can read but cannot independently confirm: the longer the silence interval stretches past the pre-silence boundary, the harder it is to interpret as a tactical pause and the easier it is to interpret as Iran’s revealed strategic equilibrium. The methodology does not move P(scenario) on calendar duration alone — per Rule 4 of the published methodology, absence of news is only weak evidence of stalemate — but the cumulative calendar evidence is now consistent with Rule B’s underlying assumption that the post-cliff regime is structurally a continuation of the pre-cliff equilibrium.

The frozen tracker’s depletion math does not move at the boundary. Iran still retains ~70% of the pre-war BM stockpile and ~40% of the pre-war drone stockpile per the Apr 19 NYT/US officials read; the effective launcher count still holds at ~200 (reconstitution plateau Day 33) per the Apr 19 ISW baseline; the days-to-functional-zero figure still sits at ~100 from any future resumption. The chart and snapshot table remain on the dashboard at their last live state precisely because the methodology cannot rule out the ~23% Rule B rupture scenario inside the rolling window. The chart is paused; it is not retired.

The Two-Endpoint Pre-Publication Test

The May 19 baseline lock-in story flagged that the methodology was about to be tested at two pre-dated endpoints: the May 20 calendar-threshold cliff and the May 25 conditional methodology revision. Today (May 26) is the first calendar day at which both endpoints have passed and the headline behavior can be read against the pre-publication.

The published commitment was: (i) the headline dial would not move at either endpoint, because the triplet of ladders spanned only ~1.1 pp of headline values; (ii) the load-bearing change would be on the rationale page, in the P(rupture-scenario) reading; (iii) the revision threshold at 00:00 UTC May 25 would fire mechanically conditional on the observed window outcome, without operator discretion. The realized behavior across both endpoints was:

  • May 20 (cliff): Headline dropped ~8.7 pp from 15% to ~6.3% in a single mechanical step — the largest single-day move of the tracker, but pre-committed and pre-explained on May 19. P(rupture-scenario) on the primary read moved 28% → 28% (Rule C identical to itself); on the weighted posterior moved 23% (pre-cliff) → 28% (Rule C cliff posterior). Headline did not move except as the pre-committed step required.
  • May 25 (revision): Headline read ~6.3% → ~6.3%: rule-invariant zero movement. P(rupture-scenario) on the primary read moved 28% (Rule C) → 23% (Rule B), exactly the pre-committed delta. Tracker A conditional liveness moved the same 5 pp. The revision fired mechanically at midnight conditional on the observed null window; no operator discretion was exercised.

The headline dial moved exactly once across the two endpoints, in the direction and magnitude the methodology had pre-committed to under timestamp. P(rupture-scenario) moved twice across the two endpoints, in the direction and magnitude the methodology had pre-committed to. Tracker A conditional liveness moved twice in lockstep with P(rupture-scenario). No published value was set retrospectively. The methodology’s designed behavior — that calendar-threshold and conditional-revision rules produce mechanical updates auditable against pre-publication — has been demonstrated end-to-end across a five-day natural experiment.

This is the methodology’s clearest evidence yet that the published triplet structure does what it was designed to do: separate the calendar-driven mechanical updates from the evidence-driven posterior shifts, route both through pre-dated rules, and produce a final read that is dated to a moment before any of the operative data was observable.

What Comes Next

The post-eviction natural-experiment rule pre-committed that the comparator ladders A and C would be dropped at the May 28 reconvergence horizon, at which point Rule B would become the single live ladder. That horizon is now ~48 hours away. Between now and then, the methodology continues to publish the triplet and to track the rule posterior weights, but the marginal information value of each additional null day is small (Rule B’s P(null) of ~0.93 means each null day only buys ~3–4 pp of additional Rule B posterior movement at this saturation level).

Beyond May 28, the dashboard will simplify. The scenario ladder will display only Rule B’s 70/23/7/0 values without the comparator A and C alongside; the rationale-page language for the IRGC Naval channel will retain only the falsification framing; the rule-posterior-weights line will be retired from the dashboard (the rules will have served their purpose as a published triplet that auditably resolved the methodology revision). The four-indicator watch list will remain. The conditional liveness of Tracker A will remain at ~23%. The 30-day rolling window will continue to advance daily.

The first calendar day after May 28 at which the dashboard reads differently from today will be the first calendar day on which an observable from the four-indicator watch list actually fires — an event that has not happened in the 49 days of ceasefire to date. Until then, the published primary read is the boring case the May 19 pre-publication committed to: ~6.3% P(reopen), 70/23/7/0 ladder, IRGC Naval channel as the falsification indicator, Tracker A conditional liveness at ~23%. The methodology has produced its first complete natural-experiment cycle (cliff → window → revision → comparator-drop) and the result is one that the pre-publication commitments anticipated.
