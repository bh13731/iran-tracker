The Negotiation Calendar: What No Round 3 Date Means for the 30-Day Probability Window

April 30, 2026 — Day 64 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
Day 23 of the ceasefire. P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, unchanged. Nine of those 15 percentage points rest on the “partial arrangement” scenario — a limited traffic deal short of a comprehensive peace. But a partial arrangement has to be negotiated, drafted, and implemented within the rolling 30-day window. With no Round 3 date announced and a minimum viable negotiation timeline that already consumes most of that window, the partial deal path faces a calendar constraint that the probability number itself does not fully reflect.

The Rolling Window

The Hormuz probability is framed as a rolling 30-day question: what is the probability Hormuz reopens to approximately normal commercial traffic within the next 30 days from today? That window moves forward every day. Today, the window runs April 30 through May 30.

The key implication: the window is not infinite. Scenarios have to resolve within those 30 days to contribute to the probability. The scenario ladder assigns a 15% chance to the “partial Hormuz traffic arrangement” scenario — a limited memorandum, flagged-vessel access, or temporary blockade modification that doesn’t require a comprehensive peace. If that scenario occurs, there is a 60% chance Hormuz reopens. The contribution to total P(reopen) is 9 percentage points — 60% of the total reopening probability.

For those 9 pp to materialize, the partial arrangement has to be successfully negotiated, agreed upon, and implemented before May 30.

The Minimum Viable Timeline

What does a partial arrangement actually require? The Islamabad pattern from Round 1 provides an empirical template:

Phase Estimated Duration What Has to Happen
Back-channel re-engagement 3–7 days Oman or China brokers renewed contact; both sides signal willingness to discuss Hormuz specifically
Round 3 announcement 2–5 days lead time Venue, delegations, agenda publicly announced; travel and logistics
Talks themselves 1–3 days Substantive session on Hormuz partial arrangement; gap-bridging
Framework drafting 5–10 days Legal text for vessel-access memorandum or blockade partial suspension; US military coordination; IRGC standing-down orders
Implementation signal 2–5 days First commercial vessels transit; IRGC non-interference confirmed; US blockade partial suspension announced
Total (minimum) ~13–30 days From cold start (no active track) to reopening

The minimum case — 13 days — assumes everything moves in parallel, no breakdown in any phase, and both sides arrive at talks already having resolved their core disagreements in back-channel. The realistic case, given Islamabad Round 1 took 8 days to produce the ceasefire text after both sides agreed to meet, is 20–30 days from the start of re-engagement.

The problem: today is April 30 and there is no re-engagement signal. No Round 3 date. No back-channel confirmation. The minimum viable timeline already fills the remaining window.

Why This Matters for the Probability

The scenario ladder is calibrated on scenario probabilities, not on the execution timeline within each scenario. When the partial arrangement scenario is assigned a 15% probability, that 15% reflects a judgment about whether such a deal will materialize within 30 days — not just whether both sides would theoretically be willing to sign one eventually.

As days of stalemate accumulate without a negotiation signal, the executable probability mass within the window decreases. The partial arrangement scenario requires an active diplomatic mechanism: a facilitator, a venue, both sides agreeing to discuss Hormuz specifically rather than comprehensive peace. None of those elements exist today. The 23-day stalemate without a Round 3 date is evidence that the mechanism has not been assembled.

This is distinct from saying the deal is impossible. It is saying that the 15% probability assigned to the partial arrangement scenario includes a time-conditioned assumption: that the negotiation cycle can be initiated and completed within the rolling window. Each day that passes without initiating that cycle reduces the plausible space in which the scenario resolves before May 30.

The structural point: The 30-day window is rolling, but the negotiation calendar is not. A partial arrangement requires sequential steps that cannot all be parallelized. At Day 23 with no active track, the minimum viable timeline (13–30 days) already fills most of the remaining window. The probability is not wrong to stay at 15% — but the decomposition is increasingly concentrated in the “miracle sprint” tail of the partial arrangement probability distribution rather than its center mass.

The Comprehensive Deal Path Is Even Tighter

If the partial arrangement path is compressed, the comprehensive peace deal path (7% scenario, 6 pp contribution) is more so. A comprehensive peace deal requires not just a Hormuz arrangement but resolution of the underlying conflict terms — Iran’s nuclear program, sanctions, regional proxies, Hezbollah disarmament. The Islamabad framework produced a ceasefire in roughly 8 days; a comprehensive peace typically takes months of sustained negotiation.

The 7% probability for a comprehensive deal in 30 days is already very low. The calendar constraint reinforces that it belongs at the single-digit edge of the distribution. The contribution is already correct at 6 pp, but it is worth noting that the 90% P(open|comprehensive deal) assumes the deal is reached in time to implement within the window — an assumption that grows harder to satisfy as the window advances without talks resuming.

What No Signal Means

The methodology note specifies: “Absence of news is weak evidence of stalemate (slightly bearish for reopening).” Day 23 with no Round 3 date, no back-channel confirmation, and no new facilitator engagement is 23 days of accumulated weak-bearish signal. The probability has not adjusted to reflect this because the source methodology requires a discrete signal to update — a deliberate calibration choice that prevents over-reaction to news silence.

But the accumulation has directional meaning. The stalemate scenario is not just the most likely outcome (55%) because it is the baseline inertia state. It is most likely because the conditions for the alternative scenarios — an active negotiation mechanism, a willing facilitator with access to both sides, a specific deliverable both parties can claim as a win — have not appeared in 23 days. Stalemate reproduces itself not through active maintenance but through the absence of the conditions needed to exit it.

What Would Change the Calendar Constraint

The watch list items most directly relevant to the calendar constraint:

  • Islamabad Round 3 date announced (credible) → +5–10 pp. This is the single most direct signal that the negotiation mechanism has been assembled. Even a Round 3 announcement without substantive pre-agreement would confirm the track exists and compress the calendar favorably.
  • Chinese or Omani direct mediation engagement → +4–7 pp. Oman has historically been the US–Iran back channel; China has economic motivation to push for a partial arrangement. Either announcing direct engagement on Hormuz specifically (not the broader ceasefire) would signal the mechanism is being assembled.
  • US/Iran back-channel confirmed active → +4–6 pp. A confirmed direct channel, even via intermediary, would bypass the Round 3 logistics timeline and could compress the negotiation calendar significantly.

None of these signals have appeared. The watch list has been static for 8 days (since the Apr 22 dual negative of postponed Round 2 and IRGC ship seizures). Eight days of watch-list silence after the Apr 22 inflection is its own data point.

Bottom Line

P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, unchanged. The probability number is stable, but its internal composition is becoming more tail-dependent. Nine of the 15 percentage points rest on a partial arrangement scenario that requires a negotiation cycle of 13–30 days from cold start — and the cold start has not begun. Each day of diplomatic silence is not neutral; it is a day consumed from the window in which the scenario must execute. The stalemate at 55% is sticky not because anyone is actively enforcing it but because the conditions for the exit paths have not assembled.
