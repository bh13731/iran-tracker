The Watch List After the Cliff: Why Half the Indicators Retire on May 20

May 16, 2026 — Day 39 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
Day 39 of cease-fire. P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, static for 18 consecutive days. Four days remain to the May 20 partial-arrangement cliff. The three prior stories priced the cliff (May 12), the scenario-redistribution rule (May 14), and the Round 3 credibility filter on the cliff-aversion path (May 15). All three treated the headline P(reopen) as the analytical object. Today flips the lens: when the partial scenario evicts on May 20, the watch list itself undergoes a phase transition. Four of the eight current indicators were calibrated to move probabilities that no longer exist after the cliff — their calibrated impact drops to zero whether or not the underlying events happen. The post-cliff watch list collapses to four operative indicators, ~75% of which are negative-side rupture or naval triggers. The May 20 cliff is therefore a one-step halving of the tracker’s upside-detection bandwidth alongside the ~8 pp headline re-anchoring — and the methodology’s capacity to surprise to the upside drops sharply at the same instant the dial moves down.

The Eight Indicators, Tagged to Scenarios

The watch list as currently maintained in source/hormuz-state.md contains eight indicators with calibrated impact values on P(reopen). Each indicator is calibrated to move either P(scenario) for one of the four scenarios, P(open | scenario) for one of them, or some combination. The first analytical step is to tag each indicator with the scenario whose ladder line it actually conditions:

8 US / Iran back-channel confirmed active +4–6 pp Partial arrangement (P(scenario)) and comprehensive (secondary) Re-weights down (~+1–2 pp residual via comprehensive)

Six of the eight indicators have at least some calibrated effect that runs through the partial-arrangement scenario. Four of those six run solely through it — their entire calibrated impact disappears when the partial scenario’s P(open | scenario) drops to zero on May 20. The four are: Round 3 date announced (1), commercial vessel transits without interference (4), Chinese/Omani mediation (5), Saudi/UAE partial support (6). Two indicators (3, 8) have partial-scenario primary impact plus a smaller residual through the comprehensive-deal scenario; they survive but at materially reduced calibrated weight. Two indicators (2, 7) carry primary impact through the rupture scenario; they survive unaffected.

The Post-Cliff Watch List

Applying the methodology’s watch-list phase-transition rule mechanically, the May 21 watch list looks like this:

Indicator Pre-cliff impact Post-cliff impact Sign
Ceasefire rupture / Iran resumes fire −13 to −15 pp −6 to −8 pp −
New IRGC ship seizures / naval incidents −5 to −8 pp −3 to −5 pp −
US carrier withdrawal / drawdown +3 to +5 pp +1 to +2 pp +
US/Iran back-channel confirmed active +4 to +6 pp +1 to +2 pp (via comprehensive) +

The post-cliff list shrinks from eight indicators to four operative ones. The negative-side aggregate weight, computed as the unsigned sum of central-case calibrated impacts, lands at roughly 11 pp (6–8 + 3–5, midpoints). The positive-side aggregate weight lands at roughly 3 pp (1–2 + 1–2, midpoints). The ratio is approximately 11:3, or ~78% of trigger weight on the negative side. The pre-cliff ratio was approximately 13:30 in the opposite direction — positive-side trigger weight dominated. The phase transition is not a marginal re-weighting; it is a sign inversion in the watch list’s aggregate posture.

Why the Numbers Compress, Not Just the Count

The trigger-value compressions in the post-cliff column are not arbitrary — they follow from the scenario probabilities after Rule C redistribution. Under smoothed Rule C, the post-cliff ladder is approximately:

Scenario Pre-cliff P(scenario) Post-cliff P(scenario) (Rule C smoothed) P(open | scenario) Contribution
Stalemate 55% ~60% 0% 0 pp
Ceasefire rupture 23% ~30% 0% 0 pp
Partial arrangement 15% 0% (evicted) N/A 0 pp
Comprehensive deal 7% ~10% 90% ~9 pp
Total 100% 100% ~9%

The post-cliff comprehensive-deal scenario probability lifts from 7% to ~10% under Rule C, and that ~10% is the entire surface area on which positive-side watch-list indicators can operate. Every positive surprise has to be filtered through “does this raise P(comprehensive deal)?” rather than “does this raise P(partial arrangement)?” The bar for a positive indicator to clear is materially higher, because comprehensive-deal scenarios require coordinated multi-party signal (verifiable nuclear/missile concessions, deal text, public Iranian acceptance) rather than the lower-bar partial-arrangement triggers (a Pakistani mediator with a Round 3 date).

The pre-cliff Round 3 indicator carried +5–10 pp because it could move both P(partial) and P(open | partial) at the same time, and the partial-arrangement scenario had a 60% conditional reopening rate sitting beneath it. Post-cliff, even if Round 3 happens on May 25, it carries roughly zero calibrated impact — the partial scenario is gone, and Round 3 mechanics do not bear on the comprehensive-deal scenario’s 90% conditional rate (a comprehensive deal’s diplomatic envelope is qualitatively different from a partial Hormuz arrangement’s envelope). A “Round 3 date announced (credible)” headline on May 25 would still be a major news event, but its calibrated effect on P(reopen) is ~0.

What this implies for May 21 onward: The methodology’s upside surprises become harder to surface, not because the underlying world is less prone to positive events, but because the calibration channels that would translate those events into probability impact have been retired. A real positive event (China announces Hormuz mediation, Iran releases the seized ships, a credible US-Iran back-channel emerges) would still register on the news side but would not produce the +4–7 pp impact the pre-cliff watch list assigned it. The methodology converts those events into +1–2 pp residual impact via the surviving comprehensive-scenario channel — or zero impact, if the channel is too indirect.

The Information-Theoretic Cost of the Phase Transition

The watch list is the methodology’s sensor array for new signal. The pre-cliff array had eight sensors with calibrated weights summing to roughly 51 pp of total directional capacity (sum of absolute calibrated impacts). The post-cliff array has four sensors with total directional capacity of roughly 14 pp. The methodology’s aggregate signal-detection bandwidth therefore drops to ~27% of its pre-cliff level on May 20, on a single day, without any underlying state change.

The compression is not symmetric. The negative-side capacity (sensors 2 and 7) drops from ~21 pp to ~11 pp — a 48% reduction. The positive-side capacity (sensors 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8) drops from ~30 pp to ~3 pp — a 90% reduction. The methodology becomes nearly blind to positive surprises while remaining moderately sensitive to negative ones. This is not a methodological flaw — it is a direct consequence of the scenario ladder: once partial arrangement evicts, the only path to a meaningful headline P(reopen) increase runs through the comprehensive-deal scenario, and comprehensive deals are evidenced through structurally rarer and higher-bar signals than partial arrangements.

What the Pre-Cliff Watch List Was Actually Doing

This decomposition retroactively clarifies what 18 days of watch-list silence actually measured. The pre-cliff list had ~30 pp of positive-side capacity available for upside surprises — meaning a single fully-firing positive indicator could move P(reopen) by 5–10 pp in a day. Eighteen days of zero positive firings, with that capacity sitting on the table, was strong evidence that positive surprises were not arriving. The methodology held P(reopen) at 15% because there was no signal, but the absence of signal was itself informative against the partial-arrangement scenario: the high-capacity sensors were not firing, which is direct evidence that the underlying partial-arrangement scenario was not progressing.

Post-cliff, an analogous “18 days of silence” carries much less information. The residual positive sensors carry only ~3 pp of total capacity, so the cost of their silence is low and the inference power against the surviving scenarios is weak. The pre-cliff silence was an active negative signal; the post-cliff silence will be near-noise.

The Compounding Effect with the Credibility Filter

Yesterday’s story flagged that the Round 3 credibility filter applies asymmetrically — positive triggers must clear five criteria, negative triggers pass through unfiltered. Today’s watch-list phase transition compounds that asymmetry along a second axis — positive triggers are also being removed wholesale from the list at the cliff, while negative triggers survive intact (at compressed weight, but operationally present).

Combining the two effects: the post-cliff watch list (a) has half as many indicators total, (b) is ~78% negative-weighted by trigger value, and (c) retains the asymmetric credibility filter on the few surviving positive indicators. The combined effect is that the methodology’s capacity to detect and price upside surprises drops from “structurally weak” pre-cliff to “structurally near-zero” post-cliff. The May 20 cliff is therefore not just a probability re-anchoring but a measurement-apparatus transition: the tracker changes from one that can register both directions of surprise (asymmetrically) to one that can register mostly only negative surprises.

What This Means for the Final 96 Hours and Beyond

Three operational consequences for how May 16–20 and May 21+ should be read:

  • The May 16–20 window is the last time positive watch-list events carry full calibrated weight. If China announces Hormuz mediation, if a Round 3 date is set, if a US-Iran back-channel is confirmed — in these four days the events still carry their pre-cliff impact values. After May 20, those same events drop to ~+1–2 pp residual at most. There is a window-closure effect on the upside watch list itself, separate from the partial-scenario cliff: positive-side observable bandwidth halves at the same moment.
  • Post-cliff, P(reopen) becomes structurally sticky on the downside. With residual positive trigger weight of ~3 pp and negative trigger weight of ~11 pp, the expected-value 30-day path for P(reopen) skews further downward, not just from the post-cliff base ~7–8% but from the asymmetric sensitivity to events thereafter. A symmetric world of mixed signals would produce a falling P(reopen) under post-cliff watch-list mechanics.
  • The watch list itself needs explicit re-baselining on May 21. The methodology must publish (i) the four operative post-cliff indicators with their re-weighted calibrated impacts, (ii) the four retired indicators with their post-cliff calibrated impacts set explicitly to zero, and (iii) the rationale for the residual weights on the two re-weighted indicators. Without that re-baselining, downstream readers will treat retired indicators as still-meaningful, mispricing news events that should no longer carry calibrated weight.

The Frozen Tracker’s Equivalent: Why Tracker A Does Not Have This Problem

The depletion tracker (Tracker A) does not undergo phase transitions of this kind because it operates on a fundamentally different epistemic structure. Tracker A indicators are observable cumulative counts (UAE BMs intercepted, ISW launcher counts, Patriot stocks remaining) tied to physical inventories that exist independent of any scenario ladder. When the depletion tracker froze on Apr 11, it froze every indicator simultaneously, but no indicator was retired — each remains valid evidence for the depletion state if and when hostilities resume. The frozen-state indicators are paused, not deleted.

The Hormuz tracker is structurally different because its indicators are scenario-conditional. The watch-list entries are not measurements of physical state but measurements of the conditional probability of one specific scenario’s execution. When the scenario stops existing as a valid execution path, the conditional probability becomes undefined, and the indicator’s calibration collapses. The phase transition on May 20 is therefore a feature of subjective-Bayesian scenario tracking, not a flaw — the cost of operating over forward-looking diplomatic claims is that the calibration channels evolve with the underlying ladder.

The symmetric insight: Tracker A’s 27-day data lag (since ISW Apr 19) is not just an inactivity signal; it is the depletion tracker’s equivalent of a structural sensor-array narrowing — the authoritative sources that would update the indicators have not published. The Hormuz tracker’s May 20 phase transition is the diplomatic-tracker analog of what the depletion tracker has been undergoing passively for 27 days: a narrowing of the channels through which new information arrives. Tracker A’s narrowing is gradual and unannounced (the ISW report just hasn’t come); Tracker B’s narrowing is discrete and methodology-enforced (the partial scenario evicts on a pre-committed date).

Bottom Line

P(Hormuz reopens within 30 days) = ~15%, unchanged for 18 consecutive days. Four days remain to the May 20 partial-arrangement cliff. The cliff is not only a re-anchoring of the headline probability (~15% → ~7–8% under smoothed Rule C) but also a phase transition of the watch list. Four of the eight current indicators were calibrated to move probabilities that no longer exist after the cliff; they retire mechanically on May 20. The post-cliff watch list collapses to four operative indicators, with ~78% of aggregate trigger weight on the negative side and only ~3 pp of residual positive-side capacity routed through the comprehensive-deal scenario.

The asymmetry the May 15 story identified — that positive triggers must clear a five-criterion credibility filter while negative triggers pass through unfiltered — compounds with today’s phase-transition observation. The post-cliff methodology has both fewer positive sensors and stricter filters on the survivors, while the negative-side architecture is largely unchanged. The May 20 cliff is therefore the moment at which the tracker’s upside-detection bandwidth halves, the headline P(reopen) re-anchors downward by ~8 pp, and the expected-value 30-day path for the months that follow tilts further negative.

For the final 96 hours, the operational consequence is concrete: any positive watch-list event that fires between May 16 and May 19 carries its full pre-cliff calibrated weight; the same event firing on May 21 or later carries roughly residual weight or zero. The window for upside surprises that move the dial materially is now narrower than the calendar suggests.
