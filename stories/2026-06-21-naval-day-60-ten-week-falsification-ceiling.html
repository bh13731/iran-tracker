At 00:00 UTC today the IRGC naval seizure clock crosses Day 60 — sixty calendar days since the Revolutionary Guard seized two commercial ships near the Strait of Hormuz on April 22, with the vessels still held, no release, and no fresh incident. At ~8.6 weeks the hold passes out of the company of the seizures that resolved quickly and into the Stena Impero (2019, ~10-week) band — the historical territory of the long Gulf detentions. Within forty-eight hours the dashboard’s load-bearing 70/23 split has been read from both ends — and neither end moves the dial.

The One Channel That Could Break the Rule

Since the May 28 reconvergence horizon the tracker has published a single live scenario ladder — Rule B, all-into-stalemate: Stalemate 70 / Rupture 23 / Comprehensive 7 / Partial 0 → P(reopen) ≈ 6.3%. Under that rule, exactly one observable can force a published reversal. A fresh IRGC seizure cluster — not continued silence, not a stale anchor, not a quiet calendar — is the single event that would falsify the all-into-stalemate read and re-elevate the 23% rupture scenario, triggering a separately-published methodology-page reversal that reinstates the comparator Rule C as primary.

That makes the IRGC naval channel the dashboard’s primary falsification indicator, and it makes its silence the most informative null the tracker carries. Most of the seven read-grid channels going quiet is unremarkable — their nulls are the modal weather under a ceasefire. But the falsification channel staying quiet is a positive test surviving. Sixty days of naval silence is sixty days of compounding base-rate evidence that the rule under which the dial is computed is holding.

Why the silence is doubly informative. The Apr 22 seizure runs in both directions at once. There has been no fresh seizure (no escalation toward rupture) and no release (no de-escalation toward a deal). The two ships sit as frozen leverage — held, but undeployed. A leverage instrument that is neither cashed in nor escalated is, with each passing week, less consistent with “the opening salvo of an escalation pattern” and more consistent with “a single-event leverage trap” — an asset Iran grabbed once and has been unable to convert into either pressure or a bargaining chip.

Where Day 60 Sits Against the Base Rates

Sixty days is not an arbitrary round number on this channel. It is a threshold in the empirical record of Iranian Gulf seizures, which cluster into a short-hold group that resolves inside ~6 weeks and a long-hold group that runs ~10–13 weeks. Day 60 (~8.6 weeks) is the point at which the Apr 22 cluster leaves the first group decisively behind and enters the second.

The Apr 22 cluster has now outlasted both ~6-week resolution windows. Past that boundary, the conditional read on the seizure changes character. Inside six weeks, a held ship is ambiguous — it could still resolve as a routine detention or escalate as the first move of a campaign. Past ten weeks, the ambiguity collapses toward the leverage-trap reading: a seizure that was going to anchor an escalation pattern would, on the historical record, have produced a second incident by now, and a seizure that was going to be traded would have been traded. Neither has happened. The base-rate prior on “Apr 22 was an escalation opener” falls as the hold lengthens, and with it the upward pressure on the 23% rupture weight.

None of It Moves the Dial — and That Is the Point

The headline does not move today, for the same structural reason it did not move yesterday. Under Rule B the rupture scenario carries P(open | rupture) = 0, so the naval channel contributes 0 pp directly to P(reopen). A softer ceiling on a zero-contribution scenario cannot lift the dial. With the partial-arrangement scenario evicted on May 20, the entire ~6.3% runs through a single channel:

7% × 90% = 6.3%. Stalemate (70%) and Rupture (23%) both contribute zero. The whole positive-side dial is the lone Comprehensive-deal path at a 90% conditional open. So a deeper read on the rupture weight — today — or the stalemate weight — yesterday — cannot move the published number. What these landmarks change is the confidence in the composition beneath the number, not the number.

What Day 60 does move is the base-rate read beneath the locked 23% rupture-scenario weight, and the same conditional-liveness reading on the frozen depletion tracker. That is the architecture working as designed: the dial is insulated from the channel that governs rupture likelihood, because the rupture scenario removes neither the US blockade nor IRGC interference and therefore opens nothing.

The Depletion-Side Companion

The naval clock has a mirror on the frozen tracker, and the two clocks split the conditional-liveness reading cleanly between them. The IRGC naval channel governs how likely a rupture is — the 23% weight. The reconstitution-plateau clock governs what posture a rupture would fire from — the ~200-launcher floor, now flat for Day 59 of published readings (the ISW Apr 19 anchor is 63 days stale, so “the plateau held 59 days” and “launchers have not been re-measured in 63 days” are the same flat line). The floor is robust to that staleness because the confound is one-sided: ceasefire-period maintenance attrition is slow and a dig-out program only adds, so the uncertainty lives in the upper tail, not the floor. Two halves of one coin, one carried on each tracker, neither touching the dial — because under Rule B P(open | rupture) is zero whatever posture the rupture would fire from.

The Clean Statement

Sixty days of naval silence is not just a round number. It is the falsification channel — the one observable that could break the rule the dial is computed under — surviving its own ten-week test. As the Apr 22 seizure crosses out of the ~6-week resolution group and into the Stena Impero long-hold band, held in both directions, the base-rate prior re-anchors from “escalation opener” toward “single-event leverage trap,” and the upward pressure on the 23% rupture weight reads as absent. Paired with yesterday’s fire-silence Day 70, the dashboard’s load-bearing 70/23 split has now been read from both ends inside forty-eight hours: durable on the Stalemate side, unpressured on the Rupture side. None of it moves the ~6.3% dial — only a comprehensive deal can — but on June 21 the falsification channel’s ten-week silence is the cleanest evidence the tracker can produce that the rupture weight is not being pushed upward, and the stalemate read is the one the evidence keeps confirming.
