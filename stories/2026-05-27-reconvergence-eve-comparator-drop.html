Reconvergence Eve: 24 Hours Before the Comparator Drop

May 27, 2026 — Day 50 Analysis Cease-fire Hormuz
The May 28 00:00 UTC reconvergence horizon is now ~24 hours away — the calendar threshold at which the dashboard’s comparator ladders (Rules A and C) are dropped per the post-eviction natural-experiment rule and Rule B (all-into-stalemate) becomes the single live ladder. This is the first dashboard architecture change since the May 20 cliff, and the third pre-committed structural transition of the post-cliff methodology cycle (after the cliff itself on May 20 and the C→B revision on May 25). Today’s headline ~6.3% is unchanged for the eighth consecutive day. Seven compounded null days since the cliff shift rule posterior weights from the cliff-day uniform 33/33/33 to roughly ~9/30/61: Rule B at ~61%, Rule A holding at ~30%, Rule C reduced to ~9%. Today is the last dashboard read on which all three rules appear side-by-side. The structural question for this story is not what the dashboard will read tomorrow — the headline is rule-invariant and will not move — but what the dashboard loses at the horizon, what it retains, and why the methodology pre-committed to the loss.

Two observations on the seventh row. The window in which a single fresh event could mechanically restore Rule C to dashboard primary has now closed. This is what the reconvergence horizon was published to recognize.

What Drops at 00:00 UTC May 28

The post-eviction natural-experiment rule, published on May 17 as the pre-commitment for the May 21–24 window, specified that the comparator ladders would be retained for a fixed calendar period after the cliff and dropped at the reconvergence horizon — the point at which the three rules’ marginal predictions become statistically indistinguishable from base rates. The horizon was set to May 28 at publication. The fixed-date pre-commitment means the drop is a calendar event, not a state-conditional one: it fires on schedule regardless of the rule-posterior trajectory.

Three things drop from the dashboard at the horizon:

  • The comparator scenario ladders. The Rule A column (proportional reweighting, 65/27/8/0 → 7.4%) and the Rule C column (smoothed asymmetric, 65/28/7/0 → 6.3%) are removed from the rationale page. The scenario ladder displays only Rule B’s 70/23/7/0 values.
  • The rule-posterior-weights line. The current dashboard publishes the rolling rule-posterior weights (~9/30/61 today) as a single auditing line. This is retired at the horizon — the rules have served their published purpose as a triplet that auditably resolved the methodology revision on May 25.
  • The dual-rule rationale language. Watch-list indicator descriptions that currently carry both Rule B and Rule C framings (e.g., “corroboration under Rule C / falsification under Rule B”) are simplified to the Rule B framing only. The IRGC Naval channel reads as a clean falsification indicator without the historical Rule C cross-reference.

What stays:

  • The four-scenario architecture. Stalemate / Rupture / Comprehensive / Partial. The partial scenario remains evicted (P(scenario) = 0%, P(open | scenario) = N/A); the four-scenario shape is the published primitive across both pre- and post-horizon dashboards.
  • The four-indicator watch list. All four operative indicators (IRGC seizure, ceasefire rupture, commercial transit cluster, comprehensive deal signal) carry over with their calibrated probability impacts unchanged. The indicator framing simplifies; the indicator set does not.
  • The 30-day rolling window. Advances daily as before.
  • The Tracker A conditional-liveness reading. Locked at Rule B’s P(rupture-scenario) of 23%; unchanged at the horizon.
  • The published source files. source/hormuz-state.md retains the full rule-triplet documentation in the historical record; only the live dashboard simplifies.

The Information Loss is Asymmetric

Dropping the comparator ladders is a real information loss for one specific kind of subsequent observable: a fresh event that would have falsified Rule B and partially restored Rule C. Under the pre-horizon dashboard, such an event is read against both rules in parallel and the posterior re-weighting is auditable through the rule-posterior-weights line. Under the post-horizon dashboard, the same event is read against Rule B alone, and the methodology has to decide whether the event triggers a Rule B falsification (which under the published rules would require a separately-published reversal of the May 25 revision) or merely a probability-impact update inside Rule B’s scenario architecture.

The methodology pre-committed to this loss precisely because the calendar exposure for a Rule C-corroborating event has now consumed roughly 87% of its window (seven of an expected ~8 days). Holding the comparator ladders past the point where their joint informational value crosses zero would degrade dashboard signal-to-noise rather than preserve auditability. The horizon is the methodology’s published threshold at which the rule-triplet structure has done its work and the marginal cost of carrying the comparators exceeds the marginal benefit.

The asymmetric piece is that the post-horizon dashboard does not lose information about a Rule B-corroborating event (continued null observation, more days of stalemate). Those events keep reading the same way; the underlying methodology continues to publish them on its source pages. The loss is specifically about Rule C-corroborating optionality, which is the optionality the seven-day null window has already structurally consumed.

The Tracker A Boundary, Day 3

Today is the third full calendar day on which Iran’s fire-silence interval is longer than the active-fire phase that preceded it (Day 46 silence vs Day 43 active fire = 107% of pre-silence duration). The interval has now compounded to roughly 7% above the boundary — small in absolute terms, but the cumulative direction is unambiguous: the silence is no longer interpretable as a tactical pause.

The frozen tracker’s depletion math does not move at the boundary. Iran still retains ~70% of the pre-war BM stockpile and ~40% of the pre-war drone stockpile per the Apr 19 NYT/US officials read; the effective launcher count still holds at ~200 (reconstitution plateau Day 34) per the Apr 19 ISW baseline; the days-to-functional-zero figure still sits at ~100 from any future resumption. The chart and snapshot table remain on the dashboard at their last live state precisely because the methodology cannot rule out the ~23% Rule B rupture scenario inside the rolling window.

What changes at the horizon is purely cosmetic for Tracker A: the conditional-liveness probability is currently published with a footnote referencing the Rule B / Rule C / Rule A weighted-posterior interpretation. After the horizon the footnote simplifies to a Rule B-only reading. The 23% figure does not change.

The Pre-Committed Cycle Completes

The methodology’s post-cliff cycle was structured as a four-phase pre-commitment: (i) cliff fires on schedule at the calendar-threshold rule (May 20); (ii) natural-experiment window opens at T+1 to T+96h (May 21–24) with the three rules’ predictions pre-published under timestamp; (iii) conditional methodology revision fires at 00:00 UTC May 25 if the window outcome supports it (it did, on a 4-day null); (iv) comparator ladders dropped at the reconvergence horizon (May 28). Phase (iv) fires tomorrow.

The cycle was designed so that every dashboard transition would be auditable against a published rule, fire on a calendar threshold rather than an operator decision, and produce a final dashboard simpler than the one that started the cycle. Each phase has now passed without operator discretion being exercised: the cliff fired mechanically on May 20; the window observation was enumerated against the seven-channel read-grid published on May 19; the revision fired mechanically on May 25 conditional on the window outcome; the comparator drop fires tomorrow on its pre-committed calendar threshold.

The post-horizon dashboard will be the simplest published version of the Hormuz tracker since the cliff: a single Rule B scenario ladder, a four-indicator watch list, a 30-day rolling window, and a Tracker A conditional-liveness footnote. The dashboard rationale page will retire roughly 40% of its current line count. The headline P(reopen 30d) read remains at ~6.3% across the transition because the headline was rule-invariant at the revision (and the comparator drop, by design, does not change a rule-invariant headline).

What Comes Next

After the horizon fires, the dashboard enters a new equilibrium with three structural properties:

  1. Single-rule architecture. All dashboard reads are Rule B reads. The next published structural transition would require either a separately-published rule revision triggered by a Rule B-falsifying event, or a fresh cliff event triggered by a calendar-threshold rule not yet on the methodology page. Neither is currently scheduled.
  2. Static base-rate reads. Under Rule B’s implied per-day P(null) of ~0.93, the base-rate expectation is that the dashboard reads ~6.3% / 70/23/7/0 / 23% Tracker A conditional liveness for an extended period punctuated only by watch-list events. A two-week stretch without any watch-list event has joint probability of roughly ~38% under Rule B; the modal post-horizon evolution is a long quiet stretch.
  3. Asymmetric event response. The four-indicator watch list is ~75% negative-side by trigger weight (three negative triggers, one positive). The post-horizon dashboard responds proportionally faster to negative events than to positive events. This is the calibrated consequence of the May 20 watch-list phase transition and is preserved through the horizon.

The next non-base-rate event of interest is the Apr 19 anchor crossing its 40-day stale mark (~May 29). That is a measurement-quality landmark, not a methodology landmark — but it is the first dashboard observable on the calendar after the horizon. The 50-day UAE anchor stale mark falls May 29 as well. After those two crossings the published dashboard is in steady state until either a watch-list indicator fires or a new methodology threshold is published.
