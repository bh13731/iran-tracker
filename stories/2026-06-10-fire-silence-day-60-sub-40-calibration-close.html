Two Landmarks, Two Different Structural Classes

The June 8 co-location — the regime’s first — paired the ISW 50-day procedural flip with the 0.9312 ≈ 42% calibration crossing. Today’s co-location is structurally distinct because the two landmarks it pairs come from two classes that have never co-located before: a compositional-quality landmark (the fire-silence Day 60 round-number mark, which shifts what a scenario means) and a calibration-quality landmark (the second-weekly-cycle close, which shifts the regime’s self-reported surprise level). Neither moves the dial; the point of the day is that they don’t, and that they fail to move it for two structurally different reasons.

The post-horizon dashboard now tracks four landmark classes, and the regime has spent three weeks demonstrating that they are mutually orthogonal in dial effect. The taxonomy — and which pairs have now co-located — is the cleanest way to read today:

What Day 60 Does to the 70% Stalemate Scenario

Fire-silence is the single most operationally load-bearing indicator the tracker carries, because it is the one variable that, if it flips, makes Tracker A live again and falsifies Rule B outright. A round-number threshold on that indicator carries more compositional weight than a threshold anywhere else. At Day 30 (May 11) the silence read as “Iran’s revealed strategic commitment.” At Day 50 (May 31) the half-century mark shifted Rule B’s 70% Stalemate scenario from “extended tactical pause inside a war that may still resume” toward “Iran’s revealed strategic preference.” Day 60 — exactly one lunar month past the half-century mark — moves the read one further notch in the same direction.

The arithmetic is the substance. 60 days of silence against a 43-day active-fire phase puts the silence at 139.5% of the interval that preceded it — the silence is now almost half again as long as the war it interrupted. The asymmetry is no longer a near-run thing the way it was at the Day 43–44 crossover (May 24–25, when silence first exceeded active fire); it is structural. The 70% Stalemate scenario does not get more probable because of this — the dial is unchanged — but the composition of that 70% tilts further toward the reading in which the blockade-plus-silence equilibrium is Iran’s preferred end-state rather than a way-station to either rupture or reopening.

Why Week 3 Is the Sharper Test

Week 2 (June 4–10) opened at 0.938 ≈ 56% and crossed both the 50% line (June 6) and the 40% line (today) inside the cycle. Its informational claim was dial-invariance under within-cycle calibration erosion plus a co-located procedural flip. The dial held across all of it. Week 3 (June 11–17) opens tomorrow at 0.9315 ≈ 34% and closes at 0.9321 ≈ 22%: it is the first weekly cycle to publish entirely sub-40% from open to close. Every day inside it sits below any prior weekly cycle’s floor; the architecture will publish through seven consecutive sub-40% calibration crossings, ending the cycle at a baseline the regime’s own model assigns barely better than one-in-five. If the dial holds through Week 3, the insulation claim will have been tested at materially sharper compounded surprise than anything the regime has carried to date — and the only events that would legitimately move it (a fresh IRGC seizure, a ceasefire rupture, a comprehensive-deal signal, a commercial-transit cluster) remain, as they have for twenty-one consecutive days, absent.
