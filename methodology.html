Iran Tracker Dashboard Analysis

Methodology & Caveats

How both trackers work, what they can and cannot tell you, and where the numbers come from.

Two Trackers, Two Questions

This site runs two co-equal trackers that address different questions about the 2026 Iran conflict:

  • Tracker B — Strait of Hormuz Reopening (LIVE): The primary live dashboard during the ceasefire period. Headline metric is P(Hormuz reopens to ~normal commercial traffic within 30 days), maintained as a calibrated Bayesian probability estimate updated daily from source/hormuz-state.md.
  • Tracker A — Missile & Drone Depletion (FROZEN): The original tracker. Clock paused when Iran's direct fire stopped on Apr 11. All depletion data is preserved exactly as of the last live day. Resumes as the primary tracker if the ceasefire ruptures.

If the ceasefire ruptures, the visual hierarchy inverts: Depletion becomes prominent and live; Hormuz becomes secondary.

Purpose — Tracker A (Depletion)

This tracker provides a current operational assessment of Iran's missile and drone capacity during the 2026 conflict. It is not a raw data dump or a news aggregator. Every number is either a confirmed floor (from official sources) or a best estimate (triangulated from multiple source tiers).

Core Principles

  1. Never confuse stockpile with launch capacity. Stockpile = missiles/drones physically held. Launch capacity = what Iran can actually fire given launchers, crews, logistics, and C2.
  2. Launcher count is the primary KPI for ballistic-missile effectiveness.
  3. Official target-country tallies are the floor, not the full total.
  4. Best estimate must be kept separate from confirmed floor.
  5. Single-source cumulative tallies never become the baseline on their own.
  6. If the model changes materially, say why. Do not silently overwrite old judgments.

How Numbers Are Built

Confirmed Floor

The sum of official cumulative intercept counts from each target country. UAE MoD figures are the highest-confidence anchor. Other Gulf states contribute medium-confidence working tallies built from official daily reports and cumulative snapshots.

Best Estimate

The confirmed floor plus adjustments for:

  • Underreporting in the Israel theater (significant but underpublished in clean cumulative form)
  • Missiles/drones that struck targets without being intercepted
  • Strikes on Jordan, Iraq, Oman, and other non-primary theaters
  • Reporting gaps between Gulf state daily updates

Launcher Estimates

Derived from IDF/ISW structured reporting on pre-war launcher counts, confirmed strikes on launch bases (29+ as of Apr 1), production facility destruction, and qualitative indicators like IRGC morale collapse and C2 decapitation (two senior Aerospace Force commanders killed within 48 hours).

Methodology Shift — Day 20 (Mar 19)

On Day 20, the tracker was restructured from a narrative log to a structured floor/best-estimate model. Pre-Day 20 data used a different methodology (less structured, wider ranges). The two series are not directly comparable, and charts mark this transition.

Known Caveats

  • Pre-Mar 19 data uses a different methodology (less structured, wider ranges)
  • Kuwait and Qatar cumulatives were significantly revised upward on Mar 23 after Wikipedia cross-reference
  • Saudi data is methodologically noisy; BM/CM split only available from Mar 20
  • Drone pre-war estimates range 30K-80K — extremely wide uncertainty
  • Launcher estimates are judgmental, combining hardware kills with C2/morale degradation
  • Production was ongoing early in the war but is assessed as halted for BMs since ~Mar 29

Output Discipline

Every update reports:

  • Confirmed floor
  • Best estimate
  • Confidence level
  • What changed in the last 24h
  • What would change the assessment next

We avoid: pseudo-precise arithmetic unsupported by data, treating destroyed launchers as destroyed missile inventory, large narrative swings from a single source family, and repeating full war history in every update.

Tracker B Methodology — Hormuz Reopening Probability

Approach

The Hormuz tracker uses a subjective Bayesian scenario decomposition. It is a calibrated human judgment, not a model output. The approach:

  1. Define 4 exhaustive, mutually exclusive scenarios that cover the full outcome space.
  2. Assign a probability to each scenario (sum to 100%).
  3. Assign P(reopen | scenario) — the probability Hormuz reopens to ~normal commercial traffic conditional on that scenario occurring within 30 days.
  4. Compute P(reopen) = ∑ P(scenarioi) × P(reopen | scenarioi).

Updating Rules

  • The estimate is updated when new signal arrives. Absence of news is weak evidence of stalemate (marginally bearish for reopening).
  • Scenario probabilities shift based on diplomatic signals, naval incidents, carrier movements, and official statements.
  • P(open | scenario) is set based on whether that scenario's resolution removes both the US blockade AND IRGC interference to approximately pre-war commercial traffic levels.
  • Confidence in the point estimate is low: ±8 pp given scenario probability uncertainty.
  • The 30-day window is a rolling window from the current date.
